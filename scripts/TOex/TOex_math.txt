===============================================================================================
 TOex_math.txt — the equations, and the order the solver asks its questions in
===============================================================================================
 Companions: TOex_workflow.txt (prose) · TOex_bench.txt (validation) · TOex_implementation.txt
 Notation: ⇒ therefore · ← assignment · ! an assumption or a place a limit binds.

 ONE SENTENCE. One unknown (the shared log₁₀fO2), one equation (the sinks' oxygen demands sum to
 the budget). Everything else defines a demand or the thermodynamics needed to evaluate it.

 ! UNITS, AND THIS CHANGED 2026-09-06. Every oxygen, carbon and hydrogen quantity below is an
   ABSOLUTE MASS IN GRAMS on a 1 kg reference cell (Mt = 10³ g). Nothing is a share of TOₑₓ any
   more. The budget appears in exactly one place — the right-hand side of (1.1) — because
   dividing by it broke twice: a NEGATIVE budget (a cell with metallic iron) inverted every sink
   bracket and froze the residual flat, and a NEAR-ZERO one inflated the shares 194x and with them
   the tolerance. A host converts once, by mass: grams × Mtot/Mt.

===============================================================================================
 PART 0 — THE FLOWCHART: what the solver asks, and what each answer makes it do
===============================================================================================

 SETUP — once per cell
 ┌ Is there any excess oxygen to place?
 │    TOₑₓ = 0 ⇒ return at once, converged, nothing partitioned.
 ├ Is there a solid? a melt?
 │    maxsOₑₓ ≤ 0 or maxmOₑₓ ≤ 0 ⇒ that sink is ABSENT, not empty. No oxybarometer exists for it,
 │    so any fO2 attributed to it would be fictitious and is suppressed everywhere downstream.
 ├ Do I have to build the solid oxybarometer table?
 │    ϕ = 1 ⇒ no solid ⇒ skip it. This is the expensive step (2 endmembers × 30 R-nodes).
 └ How tight must the residual be?
      atol ← rel × max(|TOₑₓ|, 10⁻⁶ × capacity). Grams need a scale; the budget supplies it, and
      the capacity floor stops a near-zero budget demanding impossible precision.

 BRACKET
 └ Does F change sign across [-120, +40]?
      No ⇒ no root exists anywhere ⇒ report the cell FAILED and return the closer endpoint. The
      caller keeps the cell's previous partition and never stops the run.

 EACH TRIAL f  — about 10 of them, bisection/Illinois on the bracket
 │
 ├─ SOLID IRON
 │  ┌ At the metal floor, does the rock already read ABOVE f?
 │  │    Yes ⇒ pin sOₑₓ = -minsOₑₓ. All Fe as Fe⁰; the solid has left the equilibrium and its own
 │  │    reading is legitimately ≠ f.                                  :solid_metal_saturated
 │  ├ At the ferric cap, does it still read BELOW f?
 │  │    Yes ⇒ pin sOₑₓ = +maxsOₑₓ. Every Fe²⁺ oxidised, sink full.    :solid_saturated
 │  └ Otherwise: invert the oxybarometer for the loading whose own reading IS f.
 │       fO2 equality is an IDENTITY here, not an equation to solve — see (1.2).
 │
 ├─ MELT IRON — the same two questions, no metal branch (the melt has no Fe⁰).
 │
 ├─ CARBON, FLUID AND HYDROGEN — a COMPLETE C-H solve at this f, nested inside the oxygen one
 │  ┌ Is there any carbon or hydrogen at all?
 │  │    Neither ⇒ skip the whole loop, aC ← 0, all mole fractions 0. This is the common case in a
 │  │    real run, and it saves ~78 EOS solves per cell.
 │  └ Otherwise loop (2-3 passes typical, 30 max):
 │     ┌ Does the melt want more hydrogen than the cell owns?
 │     │    Yes ⇒ UNDERSATURATED: no fluid, solve for λ ∈ (0,1] in log space (2.37).
 │     │    No  ⇒ solve the fluid/melt hydrogen split on [0, molH_tot] (2.36).
 │     ├ Is the speciation valid at the EMOG ceiling aC_cap?
 │     │    No ⇒ walk the ceiling DOWN a decade at a time until it is. Bracketing against an
 │     │    activity where the speciation does not exist cannot converge.
 │     ├ Does carbon demand at that ceiling exceed the budget?
 │     │    No  ⇒ SATURATED: sit at the ceiling, precipitate the remainder as a solid phase.
 │     │    Yes ⇒ solve log₁₀aC for exact carbon balance (3.4).
 │     ├ Did the speciation return anything non-finite?
 │     │    Yes ⇒ mark it invalid HERE. NaN passes every relational test, so a later `disc < 0`
 │     │    or `c_q ≤ 0` guard would not catch it and it would reach a product like 0 × Inf.
 │     └ Have φ, the carbon balance and the hydrogen balance all stopped moving?
 │          Yes ⇒ stop. No ⇒ another pass, updating φ from ZD09.
 │
 └─ RESIDUAL
    └ Do the demands add up to the budget?
         F(f) = sOₑₓ + mOₑₓ + carbonO + carbO + fₑₓ + mH2ₑₓ - TOₑₓ.

 UPDATE
 ┌ Is |F| within atol?                    Yes ⇒ converged.
 ├ Has the bracket collapsed with |F| still large?
 │    Then F is DISCONTINUOUS there and no root exists. Report failure — never success.
 └ Otherwise bisect and go again (≤ 500).

 AFTERWARDS
 └ What is reported?
      The state RE-DERIVED at the converged f, so what is reported is exactly what the residual
      saw. Iron speciation, buffer offsets, a regime label (a label only, never an input).

 ! CARBON AND HYDROGEN CLOSE EXACTLY AT EVERY TRIAL f, not only at the root. The outer solve does
   OXYGEN ALONE; the C-H subsystem is already balanced whenever it is asked.

===============================================================================================
 PART 1 — SYMBOLS, REFERENCE STATE, THE ONE EQUATION
===============================================================================================

 1.0  INPUTS   P [GPa] (Pbar = 10⁴P) · T [K] · p basalt fraction · ϕ MELT fraction ·
               TOex, TC, H as mass fractions of the tracer.
      MASSES on Mt = 10³ g:  Mf = ϕMt · Ms = (1-ϕ)Mt · Mc = TC·Mt · Mh = H·Mt · TOₑₓ = TOex·Mt
      THE UNKNOWN  f = log₁₀fO2.
      THE DEMANDS, all GRAMS OF OXYGEN:
        sOₑₓ    Fe³⁺ in the solid — SIGNED, negative = Fe⁰ handing oxygen back
        mOₑₓ    Fe³⁺ in the melt
        carbonO CO₂ dissolved in the melt          carbO  solid carbonate
        fₑₓ     NET of the C-O-H fluid — SIGNED    mH2ₑₓ  melt H₂, always ≤ 0
      ! ϕ (melt fraction) and φᵢ (fugacity coefficient) are different quantities.

 1.1  REFERENCE STATE — oxygen in excess of a fully REDUCED assemblage: Fe as FeO, C as graphite,
      H as H₂O. H₂O is the reference and therefore NEUTRAL.
        CO₂, carbonate +2/C · CO +1/C · gph/dia 0 · CH₄ -2/C · H₂O 0 · H₂ -1/H₂
        Fe³⁺ +0.5/Fe · Fe⁰ -1/Fe · (Fe²⁺ is the zero)
      ! The negative entries are why fₑₓ and sOₑₓ are signed and can be SOURCES.

 1.2  BOOKKEEPING, once                                                    [mass_budget]
        X = p·XB + (1-p)·XH   over {SiO2,MgO,FeO,CaO,Al2O3,Na2O,Cr2O3,O}              (0.1)
        nB_per_mass = Σⱼ XBⱼ/Mⱼ · molfracXB = (XBⱼ/Mⱼ)/nB_per_mass                (0.2)-(0.3)
        nOxideTotal ≡ molMf = Mf·nB_per_mass · nC_total = Mc/M_C                  (0.4)-(0.5)
        Φ = 2·M_O·molMf                    grams of O per unit XCO₂/(1-XCO₂)          (0.6)
        maxXCO₂ = molCav/(molMf + molCav), molCav = Mc/M_C   DIAGNOSTIC ONLY          (0.8)

 1.3  THE EQUATION                                                   [unified_residual]
        F(f) ≡ sOₑₓ + mOₑₓ + carbonO + carbO + fₑₓ + mH2ₑₓ - TOₑₓ = 0                 (1.1)
      ! WHY POSED THIS WAY. The naive form has five unknowns plus "all phases share one fO2".
        This inverts it to ONE unknown and ONE equation, and fO2 EQUALITY BECOMES AN IDENTITY:
            sOₑₓ(f) ≡ sfO2⁻¹(f)        mOₑₓ(f) ≡ melt_fO2⁻¹(f)                       (1.2)
        It can fail only for a sink pinned at a physical end — correct physics, since a phase
        holding no Fe³⁺, or all of it, no longer buffers.

 1.4  MONOTONICITY ⇒ EXISTENCE AND UNIQUENESS
        sOₑₓ, mOₑₓ  inverses of increasing oxybarometers                            (1.3a)
        carbonO     Bmelt ∝ 10^f ⇒ XCO₂/(1-XCO₂) increasing                          (1.3b)
        carbO       x_carb logistic in f                                             (1.3c)
        fₑₓ         2XCO₂ + XCO - 2XCH₄ - XH₂ shifts oxidised as f rises              (1.3d)
      ⇒ F non-decreasing ⇒ at most one root, and plain bisection suffices: no Jacobian, no guess.
      ! (1.3c) is why the EMOG step is RAMPED, not switched — a jump of 2 O per carbon lets F
        straddle zero without containing a root.
      ! (1.3a) is why a non-monotonic table must be repaired (2.3)-(2.4).
      ! THE SAME ARGUMENT FORBIDS ANY BOOLEAN INSIDE A DEMAND. One survived until 2026-09-06:
        "solid carbon exists iff aC ≥ aC_cap(1-10⁻⁹)" punched a hole in carbO wherever the aC
        solve landed a hair below its ceiling, and the bisection narrowed onto the hole. See (3.3).

===============================================================================================
 PART 2 — THE DEMAND FUNCTIONS
===============================================================================================

 2.1  SOLID IRON — a tabulated oxybarometer                        [solid_fO2_interpolant]
      Sample MAGEMin at R = Fe³⁺/ΣFe nodes on a SIGNED axis (metal branch available), and
      re-express the x-axis as the oxygen the solid holds:
        sOwtᵢ = X_O(rᵢ)·(0.5rᵢ)/(1 + 0.5rᵢ)                                          (2.1a)
        gridᵢ = sOwtᵢ · Ms                              GRAMS, never divided by TOₑₓ  (2.1)
        maxsOₑₓ = 0.5·(Ms·X_FeO/M_FeO)·M_O              every Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺             (2.2)
        minsOₑₓ = X_Fe(r=-2)·(M_O/M_Fe)·Ms              every Fe²⁺ → Fe⁰              (2.2b)
      REPAIR, because MAGEMin returns occasional dips:
        isotonic: minimise Σ(yᵢ-ŷᵢ)² s.t. ŷ non-decreasing                            (2.3)
        then a 10⁻⁶ tilt to make it STRICTLY increasing so the inverse is single-valued (2.4)
        linear extrapolation beyond the end knots, slope 1/0.1917                     (2.5)-(2.6)
      IRON SPECIATION at the answer:
        FeM_solid = metal_wt·Ms/M_Fe / nFe          FeR_solid = max(0, 2(nO+nFe⁰)/nFe)
                                                                                (2.6a)-(2.6b)

 2.2  MELT IRON — Hirschmann (2022), analytic                                 [melt_fO2]
        nOx = Mf·nB_per_mass ; nO⁺ = mOₑₓ/M_O ; θ = nOx + nO⁺                         (2.7)
        log₁₀(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) = a·f + b/T + c + Σ dᵢXᵢ + composition cross-terms            (2.8)
        maxmOₑₓ = 0.5·molfracXB_FeO·nOx·M_O                                           (2.9)

 2.3  MELT CO₂ — a carbon buffer inverted for XCO₂                    [carbon_buffer_fO2]
        f = 5.44 - 21380/T + 0.078(10⁴P-1)/T + log₁₀XCO₂                             (2.10)
        Bmelt(f) = 10^[f - 5.44 + 21380/T - 0.078(10⁴P-1)/T]                         (2.11)
        XCO₂ = softcap(aC·Bmelt, 0.3) · nC_melt = XCO₂·nOx/(1-XCO₂)             (2.12)-(2.13)
        carbonO = Φ·XCO₂/(1-XCO₂)          ≡ 2·M_O·nC_melt   grams                   (2.14)
      ! XCO₂ IS GATED ON THE MELT EXISTING. aC·Bmelt depends on P, T, fO2 alone, so ungated it
        reports the mole fraction a melt WOULD have — and painted a whole melt-free lithosphere
        at the 0.3 softcap.

 2.4  SOLID CARBON — carbonate vs graphite, RAMPED across EMOG(D)
        x_carb  = 1/(1 + exp[-(f - fE)/wEMOG]),  wEMOG = 0.25                        (2.15)
        nC_carb = x_carb·nC_solid ;  nC_gph = (1-x_carb)·nC_solid                    (2.16)
        carbO   = 2·nC_carb·M_O                                                      (2.17)

 2.5  THE C-O-H FLUID — speciation at fixed (f, aC, φ)              [coh_reaction_step]
        XCO₂ = aC·K1·fO₂/(φ_CO2·Pbar)      XCO = aC·K2·√fO₂/(φ_CO·Pbar)        (2.18)-(2.19)
      The H-bearing terms collapse to one quadratic in XH₂:
        γ = 1 - XCO₂ - XCO ;  α·XH₂² + β·XH₂ - γ = 0 ;  XH₂ = 2γ/(β+√(β²+4αγ))  (2.20)-(2.22)
        ⇒ XH₂O and XCH₄ follow, and ΣX = 1 identically.
      ! INVALID IF γ ≤ 0, the discriminant is negative, XH₂ < 0, OR ANY X IS NON-FINITE. The last
        test is not redundant: NaN fails every comparison, so it would pass the other three.
        AMOUNTS:
        molH = 2Mh/M_H2O · nH = 2XH₂O+4XCH₄+2XH₂ · n_fluid = molH/nH            (2.23)-(2.24)
        nC_fluid = n_fluid·(XCO₂ + XCH₄ + XCO)                                       (2.25)
        fₑₓ  = n_fluid·(2XCO₂ + XCO - 2XCH₄ - XH₂)·M_O    grams, SIGNED         (2.26)-(2.27)
      ! GATED ON n_fluid > 0, and NEVER CLAMPED IF NON-FINITE. With no hydrogen n_fluid is 0 and
        0 × Inf is NaN; the old ±10³ clamp turned that into a finite 1000 that passed the
        is-finite guard and put a +1000 step into F.
        The two GROSS flows O_up, O_rel are reported separately: a reducing fluid GROWS the Oₑₓ
        inventory and that source must be booked against hydrogen, not re-derived from the net.

 2.6  MELT HYDROGEN — H₂ (a sixth sink) and H₂O (neutral, but competing for the same H)
        ln K = ln K₀ + (ΔH/R)(1/T₀-1/T) - ΔV(P-P₀)/(RT),  K = X_H2/f_H2              (2.28)
        X_H2 = softcap(K·f_H2, XH2max) ; n_H2 = X_H2·nOx/(1-X_H2) ; f_H2 = X_H2(fl)·φ_H2·Pbar
                                                                                (2.29)-(2.30)
        mH2ₑₓ = -n_H2·M_O                          always ≤ 0: an H₂ is one O short of an H₂O
                                                                                     (2.31)
        Sun & Yao H₂O solubility, molecular + hydroxyl:
        lnS_H2O = a0lnP_H2O + a1lnT + a2P²/T + (a3√P + a4)X_Na2O/T                   (2.33)
        lnS_OH  = a5lnP_H2O + a6lnT + (a7 + a8X_Al2O3 + a9X_Na2O)P/T                 (2.34)
        n_H(melt) = 2(10⁻²Mf(S_H2O + S_OH))/M_H2O                                    (2.35)
      THE SPLIT, one bracketed solve, branch chosen by whether a fluid can exist at all:
        SATURATED       molH_fl + n_H(melt, λ=1) = molH_tot,  bracket [0, molH_tot]  (2.36)
        UNDERSATURATED  n_H(melt, λ) = molH_tot, molH_fl = 0,  bracket log₁₀λ ∈ [-60,0]
                                                                                     (2.37)
      ! (2.36) STAYS LINEAR. At exactly molH = 0 the carbon demand takes its no-fluid branch, an
        endpoint log space cannot represent; posing it in log turned a guaranteed sign change into
        3151 same-sign brackets per step.
      ! MELT H IS CLAMPED TO THE CELL BUDGET on the CONVERGED state only, never inside the inner
        solves: clamping the demand there forces every residual ≤ 0 and the bracket never brackets.

===============================================================================================
 PART 3 — THE CARBON ACTIVITY aC
===============================================================================================
 aC is the one quantity that makes melt CO₂, fluid carbon and solid carbon consistent at a given
 f. It is solved INSIDE each oxygen residual.

        aC_cap = min(1, 10^(fE - f))          the EMOG(D) thermodynamic ceiling        (3.1)
        D(aC)  = nC_melt(aC) + nC_fluid(aC)   what melt and fluid together demand      (3.2)

 ┌ Is the speciation valid at aC_cap?
 │    No ⇒ walk the ceiling down by decades to the highest VALID activity aC_use. The cap is an
 │    upper bound from the buffer, NOT a guarantee that a fluid exists there; bracketing against
 │    an invalid endpoint burned 100 iterations in 33 281 solves per step and returned garbage.
 ├ Is D(aC_use) ≤ nC_total?
 │    Yes ⇒ SATURATED. Sit at aC_use; the remainder is a solid phase:
 │         nC_solid = max(0, nC_total - nC_melt - nC_fluid)                            (3.3)
 │    ! NO BOOLEAN GUARD ON (3.3). max(0,·) IS the ramp: undersaturated it is max(0,0) because the
 │      solve has just made D = nC_total, saturated it is the true remainder. Continuous either
 │      way, which (1.4) requires.
 └ Otherwise solve for exact balance:  D(aC) = nC_total,  log₁₀aC ∈ [-300, log₁₀aC_use]  (3.4)
      In LOG activity on a LOG residual: at small aC demand is ∝ aC, so log-log is a straight line
      and the secant lands in ~16 evaluations instead of a fixed 120.

 THE NON-IDEALITY LOOP, around all of the above
   (a) hydrogen split (2.36)/(2.37)   (b) aC (3.4)   (c) speciate (2.18)-(2.22)
   (d) x ← x/Σx ; φ ← ZD09 — the one expensive call                                    (3.5)
        newφᵢ = fugᵢ/(xᵢ·Pbar) = exp(lnγᵢ)                                             (3.6)
 └ Have all three residuals settled — carbon share sum, hydrogen balance, and Δφ?
      Yes ⇒ exit (2-3 passes typical). Checking φ alone is not enough: the C and H residuals were
      measured at the OLD φ, and the loop ALTERNATES C and H, so either can sit at zero while the
      other still moves.

 PART 4 — THERMODYNAMIC PRIMITIVES
===============================================================================================
 4.1  HOLLAND & POWELL (1998) Gibbs energies
        ΔH = c1(T-Tr) + ½c2(T²-Tr²) - c3(1/T-1/Tr) + 2c5(√T-√Tr)                      (4.1)
        ΔS = c1ln(T/Tr) + c2(T-Tr) - ½c3(1/T²-1/Tr²) - 2c5(1/√T-1/√Tr)                (4.2)
        G(T) = G0 - (T-Tr)S + ΔH - TΔS                                                (4.3)
        ΔG_P = V0(T)·K/(K'-1)·[(1 + K'(Pbar-1)/K)^(1-1/K') - 1]         Murnaghan     (4.4)
        μC = min[G_graphite, G_diamond]      the stable reduced carbon, by G alone     (4.5)
        K3 = exp[-(μH2O-μH2-½μO2)/RT]    K4 = exp[-(μCH4-μC-2μH2)/RT]                 (4.6)
 4.2  EMOG(D) BUFFER
        2 MgCO₃ + Mg₂Si₂O₆ = 2 Mg₂SiO₄ + 2C + 2O₂                                     (4.7)
        ΔG = 2G(Fo) + 2μC + 2G(O₂) - 2G(Mag) - G(En) ;  fE = -ΔG/(2RT ln10)           (4.8)
 4.3  ZHANG & DUAN (2009) C-O-H EOS — φᵢ and the mixture molar volume
        ε = ΣᵢΣⱼ xᵢxⱼK1ᵢⱼ√(εᵢεⱼ)   K1[H2O,CO2]=0.85, K1[CH4,H2O]=0.8, else 1          (4.9)
        Pm = 3.06258806161σ³Pbar/ε ;  Tm = 154T/ε                                    (4.10)
        solve R_gas·Tm·Z/V = Pm for V ; V[cc/mol] = 1000V(σ/3.691)³             (4.11)-(4.12)
        lnγᵢ = Z - 1 - lnZ + S1 - 2S2(1 - εᵢ/ε) + 6(1-Z)(1 - σᵢ/σ)                    (4.13)
      ! THE SAME ROOT GIVES THE FLUID DENSITY: ρ = 1000·M̄/V. It comes free, and it carries no
        validity assumption the fugacity coefficients do not already carry, since Z = P·V/RT.
        Clamped to the calibration box (≤ 10 GPa), as ZD09 is.
 4.4  BUFFERS, reported only
        FMQ: 3fa + O₂ = 3qtz + 2mag      IW: 4Fe + 2O₂ = Fe₄O₄                        (4.14)
      ! dFMQ IS MEANINGLESS AT LOW T. Measured: fO2 = -41.6 at 305 K reads dFMQ = +38.5 because
        the BUFFER is at -80 there. The reference is what is extrapolated, not the solution.
 4.5  SOFT LIMITERS   softmin2(a,b,β) = -softmax2(-a,-b,β) — used so caps never introduce a corner.

===============================================================================================
 PART 5 — WHERE THE LIMITS BIND
===============================================================================================
 A limit being touched is a BRANCH, not an error. Only the last row is a failure.

  solid at metal floor   sfO2(-minsOₑₓ) ≥ f    sOₑₓ = -minsOₑₓ, fₛ ≠ f legitimately
  solid at ferric cap    sfO2(maxsOₑₓ) ≤ f     sink full
  melt floor / cap       same, no metal branch  :melt_depleted / :melt_saturated
  both Fe caps           both saturated         the rock's capacity binds the whole budget
  phase absent           maxOₑₓ ≤ 0             ABSENT: no oxybarometer, so no fO2 is attributed
  XCO₂ at the softcap    aC·Bmelt > 0.3         the cell reports the CAP, not the chemistry
  aC ceiling invalid     speciation fails       walk down to aC_use, bracket against that
  fluid non-finite       any X not finite       invalid speciation, propagated — never clamped
  NO SIGN CHANGE         F(-120)·F(+40) > 0     FAILURE: no root. Keep the previous partition,
                                                count it, never stop the run.

===============================================================================================
 PART 7 — THE SURFACE BOX: Θ, AND HOW ONE NUMBER CARRIES BOTH CARBON AND HYDROGEN
===============================================================================================
 7.1  Oₑₓ IS AN ELEMENT COUNT, NOT A SPECIATION. From the reference state (Fe as FeO, C as
      graphite, H as H₂O):

        Oₑₓ ≡ n_O − n_H/2                                                              (7.1)

      Check every carrier: CO₂ 2−0 = +2 · CO +1 · C(gr) 0−0 = 0 · CH₄ 0−2 = −2 · H₂O 1−1 = 0 ·
      H₂ 0−1 = −1 · O₂ +2. The whole table is reproduced by one subtraction.
      ! THE −n_H/2 IS HYDROGEN'S WATER BILL, PAID UP FRONT. Oₑₓ is the oxygen that remains after
        EVERY hydrogen atom has already been given the ½ O it needs to be H₂O. That is the whole
        answer to "how does one number track carbon and hydrogen at once": hydrogen's demand is
        not tracked alongside Oₑₓ, it is SUBTRACTED INSIDE IT.
      ! ⇒ EVERY ATOM-CONSERVING REACTION IS THE IDENTITY ON (n_C, n_H, Oₑₓ). CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ +
        2H₂O is 4−2 = 2 on both sides; 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O is 2−2 = 0 on both. Chemistry inside the
        box is invisible; only TRANSPORT ACROSS ITS BOUNDARY moves Oₑₓ.

 7.2  Θ ADDS CARBON'S BILL. Per carbon atom, CO₂ costs 2:

        Θ ≡ Oₑₓ/n_C − 2                    ⇒   Θ·n_C = Oₑₓ − 2n_C                      (7.2)

      so Θ·n_C is the oxygen left over once BOTH bills are paid — hydrogen's inside (7.1), carbon's
      by the −2. Three regimes, and Θ is a RATIO so it is undefined on an empty box:

        Θ > 0   both paid, surplus is free O₂:  n_O2 = Θ·n_C/2
        Θ = 0   exactly CO₂ + H₂O, nothing spare
        Θ < 0   cannot pay both. DEFICIT = |Θ|·n_C oxygen atoms.

 7.3  WHO GOES WITHOUT. Split each carrier's Oₑₓ between its carbon and its hydrogen. CH₄'s −2 is
      0 (its CARBON is graphite-like) plus 4 × (−½) (its hydrogen); H₂'s −1 is 2 × (−½); H₂O's 0
      is 2 × 0. So hydrogen contributes −½ per atom in ANY reduced carrier and 0 in water, and
      carbon contributes +2 as CO₂ and 0 otherwise. With C_ox + C_red = n_C and H_red the hydrogen
      in CH₄ or H₂:

        Oₑₓ = 2·C_ox + 2·n_O2 − ½·H_red                                                (7.3)

      Substituting into (7.2) at n_O2 = 0 gives the identity the whole surface model rests on:

        Θ·n_C = −(2·C_red + ½·H_red)                                    MASTER          (7.4)

      IN WORDS: the deficit is paid by REDUCED CARBON at 2 apiece and by REDUCED HYDROGEN at ½
      apiece. That is how Θ carries both at once — not as two tracked amounts, but as ONE deficit
      with TWO possible payers.
      ! ONE EQUATION, TWO UNKNOWNS ⇒ Θ FIXES THE TOTAL AND NOT THE SPLIT. C_red and H_red trade
        off along (7.4) at fixed Θ, so any rule that names them is a CHOICE, not a derivation.
        CH₄ and H₂ are indistinguishable here — both are −½ per H — so the CH₄/H₂ ratio is pure
        gauge: 0.5 CH₄ + 0.5 C(gr) and 1 C(gr) + 1 H₂ are the SAME (n_C, n_H, Oₑₓ).

 7.4  Θ = −4x, THE CO₂/CH₄ ENDMEMBER RULE. Let carbon be only CO₂ or CH₄ and hydrogen only CH₄ or
      H₂O. Then all reduced carbon is CH₄, which fixes H_red = 4·C_red. With C_red = x·n_C, (7.4):

        Θ·n_C = −(2x·n_C + ½·4x·n_C) = −4x·n_C      ⇒     Θ = −4x                      (7.5)

      equivalently, directly:  Oₑₓ = n_C[(1−x)(+2) + x(−2)] = n_C(2 − 4x) ⇒ Θ = −4x.
      ! THE 4 IS THE CO₂ → CH₄ SWING, +2 to −2. Half of it (2) is the carbon leaving CO₂, half
        (4 H × ½) is the hydrogen it takes with it. This is the split already in
        toex_degas_species as xCO2 = (r_deg + 2)/4, with r_deg = Oₑₓ/n_C = Θ + 2.
      ! x = 1 AT Θ = −4 SATURATES THE CARBON. Below that only H_red can pay, and since free H₂ has
        no carbon, Θ IS UNBOUNDED BELOW: CH₄ + n H₂ sits at Θ = −4 − n. −4 is the CARBON-ONLY
        floor, not a floor on Θ.

 7.5  THE MINIMISE-H_red RULE, used for the surface box. Water is the most stable hydrogen carrier
      at surface T, so carbon pays first — and graphite pays 2 apiece while costing no hydrogen:

        C_ox  = min(n_C, max(Oₑₓ, 0)/2)      C_red = n_C − C_ox                         (7.6)
        n_O2  = max(Oₑₓ − 2·n_C, 0)/2                                                   (7.7)
        H_red = max(−2·Oₑₓ, 0)               H_wat = n_H − H_red                        (7.8)

      Closes (7.3) exactly on all three branches. NO HYDROGEN-AVAILABILITY BRANCH: feasibility is
      H_red ≤ n_H ⟺ Oₑₓ ≥ −n_H/2 ⟺ n_O ≥ 0, which is physical, not extra.
      ! ⇒ H_red > 0 ONLY WHERE Oₑₓ < 0, i.e. Θ < −2. Under this rule the air holds no H₂ until the
        excess oxygen is literally negative; under (7.5) reduced H appears as soon as Θ < 0. Same
        state, different reported speciation — 7.3's gauge freedom made visible.

 7.6  BURIAL, AND WHY IT NEEDS NO SPECIATION. Two independent scalars (B.11), with the
      availability floor f_eff and r ≡ Oₑₓ/n_C = Θ + 2:

        dO = 2(1 − f_eff)·dC          f_eff = min(max(f_org, 1 − r/2), 1)               (7.9)

      At the floor, 1 − f_eff = r/2 = Oₑₓ/(2n_C), and for Θ ≤ 0 that is exactly C_ox/n_C of (7.6):

        1 − f_eff  =  C_ox/n_C                                                         (7.10)

      ! THE AVAILABILITY FLOOR IS THE BOX'S OWN CO₂ FRACTION. Carbonate can only be made from
        carbon that is actually CO₂. (7.9) is the FLUX projection of that statement and (7.6) is
        the INVENTORY projection; they agree identically, verified across Θ ∈ [−1.5, +0.5].
      ! AND BURIAL MOVES CARBON AND OXYGEN AND NEVER HYDROGEN ⇒ no burial ratio, however tuned,
        carries information about the H₂O/H₂ split. That comes only from (7.8).
