 TOex_math.txt — the equations of src/TOex.jl and the exact order of operations
===============================================================================================
 Companions: TOex_workflow.txt (prose walkthrough), TOex_bench.txt (validation),
             TOex_implementation.txt (the StagYY port).
 Notation: [:NNN] line in src/TOex.jl (indicative) · ⇒ therefore · ← assignment · ? branch
           ! physical assumption or a place a limit binds.

 ONE SENTENCE: one unknown (the shared log₁₀fO2), one equation (five sinks' oxygen demands sum to
 the budget). Everything else is a definition of one demand or the thermodynamics to evaluate it.

===============================================================================================
 PART 0 — NOTATION, UNITS, REFERENCE STATE
===============================================================================================

 0.1  SYMBOLS
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   INPUTS
     P          pressure                                    GPa      (Pbar = 10⁴·P internally)
     T          temperature                                 K
     p          basalt fraction of the solid                0-1
     ϕ          MELT FRACTION                               0-1
     TOex       excess-oxygen budget                        mass fraction of the tracer
     TC         total carbon                                mass fraction of the tracer
     H          free water                                  mass fraction of the tracer

   ! ϕ (melt fraction) and φᵢ (fugacity coefficient) are different quantities; the code uses both.

   MASSES (Mt = 10³ g reference cell mass, so mm.* in g/mol stay consistent)
     Mf = ϕ·Mt   Ms = (1-ϕ)·Mt   Mc = TC·Mt   Mh = H·Mt
     TOₑₓ = TOex·Mt         the budget IN GRAMS — the denominator of everything

   THE UNKNOWN      f = log₁₀ fO2,  fO₂ = 10^f in mass-action terms

   THE FIVE DEMANDS — each a DIMENSIONLESS FRACTION OF TOₑₓ
     sOₑₓ      excess O held as Fe³⁺ in the solid — SIGNED, negative = Fe⁰ giving oxygen back
     mOₑₓ      excess O held as Fe³⁺ in the melt
     carbonO   excess O as CO₂ dissolved in the melt
     carbO     excess O as solid carbonate
     fₑₓ       NET excess O held by the C-O-H fluid — SIGNED

 0.2  THE REFERENCE STATE
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Oxygen in excess of a fully REDUCED assemblage: Fe as FeO, C as graphite/diamond, H as H₂O.
   ! H₂O is the reference and therefore NEUTRAL; counting its oxygen would double-count it.

   Per-species excess oxygen:
       CO₂, carbonate  +2/C     CO  +1/C     gph/dia  0     CH₄  -2/C     H₂O  0     H₂  -1/H₂
       Fe³⁺  +0.5/Fe            Fe⁰  -1/Fe            (Fe²⁺ is the zero)

   ! The negative entries are why fₑₓ and sOₑₓ are signed and can be SOURCES: reducing H₂O to
     CH₄/H₂, or FeO to Fe⁰, strips oxygen and hands it to the rest. Rock shares then exceed 1
     while all five still sum to 1.

 0.3  MASS AND MOLE BOOKKEEPING, once before the solve   [mass_budget:155]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       X = p·XB + (1-p)·XH        over {SiO2,MgO,FeO,CaO,Al2O3,Na2O,Cr2O3,O}          (0.1)

   Melt takes the BASALTIC end-member XB:
       nB_per_mass = Σⱼ XBⱼ/Mⱼ                       mol oxide per gram               (0.2)
       molfracXB   = (XBⱼ/Mⱼ)/nB_per_mass            mole fractions, Σ = 1            (0.3)
       nOxideTotal ≡ molMf = Mf·nB_per_mass          mol oxide in the melt            (0.4)
       nC_total = Mc/M_C                                                              (0.5)
       Φ        = 2·M_O·molMf/TOₑₓ                                                    (0.6)

   ! Φ converts a melt CO₂ MOLE FRACTION into a FRACTION OF TOₑₓ. With
     nC_melt = XCO₂·molMf/(1-XCO₂) and 2 O per carbon,
         carbonO = 2·M_O·nC_melt/TOₑₓ = Φ·XCO₂/(1-XCO₂)                               (0.7)

       maxXCO₂ = molCav/(molMf + molCav),   molCav = Mc/M_C                           (0.8)
   ! (0.8) is DIAGNOSTIC ONLY, never imposed — carbon balance is enforced exactly by the aC
     solve (PART 3). Guarded against 0/0 when there is neither melt nor carbon.

===============================================================================================
 PART 1 — THE ONE EQUATION
===============================================================================================

 1.1  STATEMENT                                     [_coh_derive:689, unified_residual:712]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       F(f) ≡ sOₑₓ(f) + mOₑₓ(f) + carbonO(f) + carbO(f) + fₑₓ(f) - 1 = 0             (1.1)

 1.2  WHY POSED THIS WAY
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The naive form has five unknowns plus "all phases share one fO2". This inverts it: ONE unknown,
   ONE equation. The consequence is structural —

     fO2 EQUALITY IS AN IDENTITY, NOT AN EQUATION. An unsaturated sink is DEFINED as the loading
     whose own oxybarometer reads f:
         sOₑₓ(f) ≡ sfO2⁻¹(f)          mOₑₓ(f) ≡ melt_fO2⁻¹(f)                        (1.2)
     so sfO2(sOₑₓ) = melt_fO2(mOₑₓ) = f by construction. It can fail ONLY for a sink pinned at a
     physical end — correct physics: a phase holding no Fe³⁺, or all of it, no longer buffers.

 1.3  MONOTONICITY ⇒ EXISTENCE AND UNIQUENESS
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
     sOₑₓ, mOₑₓ    inverses of increasing oxybarometers ⇒ increasing                  (1.3a)
     carbonO       Bmelt ∝ 10^f ⇒ XCO₂ increasing ⇒ XCO₂/(1-XCO₂) increasing          (1.3b)
     carbO         x_carb is a logistic in f ⇒ increasing                             (1.3c)
     fₑₓ           2XCO2 + XCO - 2XCH4 - XH2 shifts oxidised as f rises               (1.3d)

   ⇒ F non-decreasing ⇒ at most one root, and F(-120) < 0 < F(40) whenever the capacity exists.
     Hence plain bisection: no Jacobian, no guess, no divergence.
   ! (1.3c) is why the EMOG(D) step is RAMPED, not switched: a hard switch puts a jump of 2 O per
     carbon into F, and a discontinuous F can straddle zero without containing a root.
   ! (1.3a) is why a non-monotonic MAGEMin table must be repaired (2.1.3) — a dip makes the
     inversion multivalued and destroys the root.

===============================================================================================
 PART 2 — THE FIVE DEMAND FUNCTIONS
===============================================================================================

 2.1  SOLID Fe³⁺ / Fe⁰  →  sOₑₓ                        [solid_fO2_interpolant:96]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 2.1.1  TABULATED, not analytic; built once per (P,T,p) on a SIGNED axis:

     rᵢ = Rasinhspace(-1.96, Rmax, nr+nrm; R0)   Fe³⁺/ΣFe, signed   nr=100, nrm=15, Rmax=0.4
     Xᵢ = oxidize_bulk(X, rᵢ)                    Fe + O basis (an FeO + O basis double-counts
                                                 the iron's own oxygen and cannot express r < 0)
     sOwtᵢ = X_O(rᵢ)·(0.5rᵢ)/(1 + 0.5rᵢ)         excess-O mass fraction at that r    (2.1a)
     fO2ᵢ  = MAGEMin(10P, T-273.15, Xᵢ)          sb24
     metalᵢ = Σ ph_frac_wt over {feg, fea, fee}  native iron, wt fraction of the solid

     Abscissa re-expressed as a fraction of the budget, which is what makes the table directly
     invertible for a demand:
       gridᵢ = sOwtᵢ · (Ms/TOₑₓ)                                                      (2.1)

   ! r ∈ [-2, 1]: 1 all ferric, 0 the FeO reference, -2 all metal. Not symmetric — Fe²⁺→Fe³⁺
     takes half an O per iron, Fe²⁺→Fe⁰ returns a whole one — so iron carries nFe(1 + r/2) oxygen
     throughout and the excess is 0.5·r·nFe. asinh spacing is used because the logit is undefined
     at r ≤ 0 and cannot carry the reducing half at all.

 2.1.2  STOICHIOMETRIC CEILING AND FLOOR — the true limits, independent of MAGEMin's sampling:
       maxsOₑₓ = 0.5·(Ms·X_FeO/M_FeO)·M_O / TOₑₓ            every Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺, 0.5 O each  (2.2)
       minsOₑₓ = X_Fe(r=-2)·(M_O/M_Fe)·(Ms/TOₑₓ)            every Fe²⁺ → Fe⁰, 1 O each     (2.2b)
     so sOₑₓ ∈ [-minsOₑₓ, +maxsOₑₓ], with minsOₑₓ ≈ 2·maxsOₑₓ by the asymmetry above.

 2.1.3  MONOTONICITY REPAIR [_isotonic:68]. MAGEMin can return fO2 non-monotonic in r where the
     assemblage changes. Repaired by pool-adjacent-violators isotonic regression, the least-squares
     non-decreasing fit, so ordered points are untouched:
       minimise Σᵢ (yᵢ - ŷᵢ)²   subject to  ŷ₁ ≤ ŷ₂ ≤ … ≤ ŷₙ                          (2.3)
     Pooling produces TIES, and a FLAT segment is as fatal as a dip — the loading reading a given
     fO2 stops being unique — so a negligible increasing ramp breaks them:
       ŷᵢ ← ŷᵢ + 10⁻⁶·span·(i-1)/(n-1),   span = max(ŷₙ - ŷ₁, 1)                      (2.4)
     Metal needs no repair: it is monotonically DEcreasing in the loading by construction.

 2.1.4  THE OXYBAROMETER, with physical behaviour at both ends [sfO2:169]:
     g₁,f₁ = first knot, g_N,f_N = last, slope = 1/0.1917, R_N = g_N/maxsOₑₓ           (2.5)

                 ⎧ f₁                                                    s < g₁
       sfO2(s) = ⎨ interp(s)                                        g₁ ≤ s ≤ g_N       (2.6)
                 ⎩ f_N + slope·[log₁₀(R/(1-R)) - log₁₀(R_N/(1-R_N))]     s > g_N
                                                       with R = min(s/maxsOₑₓ, 1-10⁻¹²)

   ! THE TWO ENDS ARE DIFFERENT PROBLEMS. Below the table, FLAT is the physics: Fe⁰ + ½O₂ = FeO
     buffers at a FINITE fO2 however much more metal is made. Above it, fO2 must keep climbing and
     diverge at the STOICHIOMETRIC limit (2.2), hence the R/(1-R) form.
   ! slope = 1/0.1917 is BORROWED — the Hirschmann (2022) MELT exponent (2.8) — because deriving
     MAGEMin's own dilute-limit slope needs a full treatment of Fe³⁺ substitution in whichever
     solid solution sb24 uses. Justification is that every Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ oxybarometer of this family
     is a mass-action law for the same couple and so becomes log-linear far from saturation. What
     matters more is that it is correctly signed and non-flat; _invert_demand checks both
     endpoints first, so it is read only when the root falls outside the table.
   ! ϕ = 1 (no solid): Ms = 0 collapses (2.1) to one knot at zero. MAGEMin is skipped and the sink
     is flagged ABSENT.

 2.1.5  IRON SPECIATION AT THE SOLVED LOADING [:877]. Metal comes from the ASSEMBLAGE, not from
     sOₑₓ, so ferric and metal can both be non-zero at once:
       nO      = TOₑₓ·sOₑₓ/M_O                    net excess O in the solid
       FeM_solid = metal_wt·Ms/M_Fe / nFe         Fe⁰/ΣFe, total, from MAGEMin        (2.6a)
       FeM_net   = max(0, -nO/nFe)                Fe⁰/ΣFe the oxygen budget can see
       FeR_solid = max(0, 2(nO + nFe⁰)/nFe)       nFe³⁺ = 2(nO + nFe⁰)                (2.6b)
       FeM_diss  = min(FeM_net, FeM_solid)        made by FeO → Fe⁰ + ½O₂
       FeM_disp  = FeM_solid - FeM_diss           made by 3Fe²⁺ → 2Fe³⁺ + Fe⁰

   ! THE SPLIT IS EXACT, not a partition guess. Dissociation frees exactly 1 O per Fe⁰ and that O
     IS -sOₑₓ, so the net coordinate measures dissociated metal alone; the rest was made at
     constant oxygen. Disproportionation is the NULL DIRECTION of sOₑₓ — 2 Fe³⁺ consume exactly
     the 1 O that 1 Fe⁰ frees — which is why it needs the assemblage and cannot drive the solve.
   ! (2.6b) is why FeR_solid is not just 2nO/nFe: with metal present that form briefly reported
     LESS ferric than metal. It gives the 2:1 ratio at nO = 0 and zero when metal is exactly paid
     for by released O.

 2.2  MELT Fe³⁺  →  mOₑₓ                                        [melt_fO2:189]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Hirschmann (2022):  a=0.1917  b=-1.961  c=4158.1  ΔCp=33.25  T₀=1673.15
                       y1=-520.46  y3=494.39  y4=1838.34  y5=2888.48  y8=-1245.09  y9=-1156.86

       nOx = Mf·nB_per_mass ;  nO⁺ = TOₑₓ·mOₑₓ/M_O ;  θ = nOx + nO⁺                   (2.7)
       XO  = nO⁺/θ ;           Xⱼ = molfracXBⱼ·nOx/θ

       a·f = log₁₀[ XO/(XFeO - 2·XO) ] - b - c/T
             + (ΔCp/(R·ln10))·[1 - T₀/T - ln(T/T₀)]
             + IDV/(10⁻³·R·T·ln10)
             - (1/T)·[ y1·XSiO2 + y3·XMgO + y4·XCaO + y5·XNa2O
                       + y8·XSiO2·XAl2O3 + y9·XSiO2·XMgO ]                            (2.8)

   ! XO/(XFeO - 2XO): each Fe³⁺ carries 0.5 O, so XO oxygen consumes 2·XO of the ferrous pool.
     As XO → 0.5·XFeO the pool empties and f → +∞; guarded by a sentinel return of +14.
       maxmOₑₓ = 0.5·molfracXB_FeO·nOx·M_O / TOₑₓ                                     (2.9)
   ! No metal branch: a silicate melt's floor is lowclip, not -minsOₑₓ.
   ! ϕ = 0: Mf = 0 ⇒ XFeO = 0 ⇒ the sentinel fires for any loading; maxmOₑₓ = 0 ⇒ ABSENT.

 2.3  MELT CO₂  →  carbonO                     [carbon_buffer_fO2:217]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       f = 5.44 - 21380/T + 0.078·(10⁴P - 1)/T + log₁₀ XCO₂                          (2.10)
       Bmelt(f) = 10^[ f - 5.44 + 21380/T - 0.078·(10⁴P - 1)/T ]                     (2.11)
       XCO₂    = softcap(aC·Bmelt, XCO2max),      XCO2max = 0.3                      (2.12)
       nC_melt = XCO₂·nOx/(1 - XCO₂)                                                 (2.13)
       carbonO = Φ·XCO₂/(1 - XCO₂)              ≡ 2·M_O·nC_melt/TOₑₓ  by (0.7)       (2.14)

   ! XCO2max = 0.3 is the least constrained number here. Without it (2.11) drives XCO₂ → 1 near
     the carbonate boundary, where (2.13) diverges and a vanishing melt swallows all the carbon.

 2.4  SOLID CARBONATE  →  carbO
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       x_carb  = 1/(1 + exp[-(f - fE)/wEMOG]),   wEMOG = 0.25                        (2.15)
       nC_carb = x_carb·nC_solid ;  nC_gph = (1 - x_carb)·nC_solid                   (2.16)
       carbO   = 2·nC_carb·M_O/TOₑₓ                                                  (2.17)

   ! Two reasons for the ramp: NUMERICAL — the univariant boundary is a step of 2 O per carbon in
     F, and a step can straddle zero without containing a root; PHYSICAL — real carbonate is a
     magnesite-dolomite-calcite solution, so the transition is genuinely smeared.
     Carbonate is nevertheless approximated as pure MgCO₃ at unit activity.

 2.5  C-O-H FLUID  →  fₑₓ                    [_coh_reaction_step:378, coh_state:439]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2.5.1  Four mass-action reactions, K from HP98 (4.1-4.3):
       K1: C + O₂ = CO₂    K2: C + ½O₂ = CO    K3: H₂ + ½O₂ = H₂O    K4: C + 2H₂ = CH₄

 2.5.2  SPECIATION at fixed φᵢ and aC. Carbon species follow directly, aC and fO₂ both known:
       XCO₂ = aC·K1·fO₂ /(φ_CO2·Pbar)                                                (2.18)
       XCO  = aC·K2·√fO₂/(φ_CO ·Pbar)                                                (2.19)

     Hydrogen species close the system. With
       α = aC·K4·φ_H2²·Pbar/φ_CH4        β = K3·φ_H2·√fO₂/φ_H2O + 1
       γ = 1 - XCO₂ - XCO                                                            (2.20)
     the closure Σx = 1 with XCH4 = α·XH2², XH2O = (β-1)·XH2 gives
       α·XH2² + β·XH2 - γ = 0                                                        (2.21)
       XH2  = 2γ/(β + √(β² + 4αγ))                                                   (2.22)
       XH2O = (β - 1)·XH2 ;   XCH4 = α·XH2²

   ! α, β, γ are just the quadratic's coefficients (the code's `aq2`/`aq1`/`c_q`), not named
     thermodynamic quantities.
   ! (2.22) rather than (-β+√·)/(2α): dividing by α cancels catastrophically as aC → 0 and returns
     0 instead of the correct linear limit γ/β.
   ! γ ≤ 0 or β² + 4αγ < 0 ⇒ carbon species alone would overfill the fluid. The step returns
     invalid and _solve_aC responds by LOWERING aC (PART 3) rather than failing.
   ! Σx = 1 holds by construction, not by renormalisation — that is what (2.20) encodes.

 2.5.3  EXTENSIVE AMOUNTS — the fluid is sized by the hydrogen it must contain:
       molH = 2·Mh/M_H2O ;  nH = 2XH2O + 4XCH4 + 2XH2 ;  n_fluid = molH/nH      (2.23)-(2.24)
       nC_fluid = n_fluid·(XCO₂ + XCH4 + XCO)                                        (2.25)

 2.5.4  NET EXCESS OXYGEN — the 0.2 convention applied species by species:
       netO = n_fluid·(2XCO₂ + XCO - 2XCH4 - XH2)·M_O/TOₑₓ                           (2.26)
       fₑₓ  = clamp(netO, ±10³)                                                      (2.27)

   ! The sign is the point: oxidised fluid (CO₂,CO) CONSUMES budget, reduced fluid (CH₄,H₂)
     RELEASES oxygen taken off the H₂O reference. |fₑₓ| > 1 already exceeds the whole budget;
     the clamp only keeps the outer bracket finite.
   ! The two GROSS flows are reported separately (O_up, O_rel), because the net alone cannot tell
     a host model how much oxygen H₂O dissociation ADDED — a source owed to the H reservoir.

 2.5.5  FUGACITY COEFFICIENTS, Zhang & Duan (2009) — see 4.5. φᵢ = fᵢ/(xᵢ·Pbar).

===============================================================================================
 PART 3 — THE CARBON SUBSYSTEM: the activity aC
===============================================================================================

 3.1  WHAT aC IS
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   aC multiplies EVERY carbon-bearing reaction — (2.18), (2.19), the α of (2.20), the melt buffer
   (2.12) — so it scales the whole carbon system at once. It exists because oxygen balance (1.1)
   does not by itself conserve CARBON.
       aC = aC_cap   ⇒ a solid carbon host IS present, holding what melt and fluid leave over
       aC < aC_cap   ⇒ NO solid carbon; every atom is dissolved in melt + fluid

   ! ACTIVITY IS NOT AN AMOUNT. aC is an INTENSITY — carbon's escaping tendency referenced to pure
     graphite/diamond — and says nothing about how much carbon exists; that is nC_solid (3.3).

 3.2  THE CEILING
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       aC_cap = min(1, 10^(fE - f))                                                  (3.1)
     Below EMOG(D) the host is graphite/diamond, activity 1 by definition; above it the host is
     carbonate, whose own equilibrium fixes aC = 10^(fE - f). Continuous at f = fE.

   DERIVATION — falls out of (4.7) once carbon is allowed a general activity. Holding carbonate
   and both silicates at unit activity:
       ΔG_rxn = ΔG° + 2RT·ln(aC·fO2)                                                 (3.1a)
     At equilibrium ΔG_rxn = 0, and fE ≡ -ΔG°/(2RT ln10) by (4.8), so
       fE = log₁₀(aC) + f    ⇒    aC = 10^(fE - f)                                   (3.1b)
     The slope of exactly 1 is forced by stoichiometry: C and O₂ carry equal coefficients (2 and
     2) in (4.7), so log(aC) and -f trade off 1:1.

   ! WHY aC MUST FALL (Le Chatelier): raising fO2 past fE pushes (4.7) backward, consuming free
     carbon to make more carbonate. Any carbon activity consistent with carbonate + silicates at
     that fO2 is FORCED down by exactly this amount; it is not a modelling choice.
   ! WHY aC PERSISTS ABOVE fE, WHERE THE HOST IS CARBONATE: K1, K2, K4 are built from HP98's
     GRAPHITE Gibbs energy unconditionally — there is no second carbonate-referenced set. Above fE
     aC_cap is a FORMAL BRIDGING VARIABLE ("the graphite-referenced carbon fugacity this
     carbonate-buffered environment is consistent with"), which is what lets one set of equations
     serve continuously on both sides of EMOG(D).

 3.3  THE CARBON DEMAND FUNCTION                                 [_carbon_demand:408]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       D(aC) = nC_melt(aC) + nC_fluid(aC)                                            (3.2)
     Increasing in aC — checked by direct evaluation over aC ∈ [10⁻²⁰,1]: strictly non-decreasing,
     ~5×10⁻²¹ up to ~0.22.

   ! D IS A SOLUBILITY LAW: how much carbon melt+fluid would hold if buffered at activity aC, and
     D(aC_cap) is how much at SATURATION. nC_total ≥ D(aC_cap) ⇒ genuine excess, precipitates as
     solid (the solid's mass does not feed back on the activity, as a crystal's size does not
     change a solution's saturation concentration). nC_total < D(aC_cap) ⇒ saturating melt+fluid
     would need more carbon than exists, so no leftover, and monotonicity makes D(aC) = nC_total
     the only self-consistent state. The activity is forced down by insufficiency; the demand
     falls as a CONSEQUENCE.
   ! Two load-bearing guards: molH ≤ 0 ⇒ return nC_melt alone (with no free water there is no
     fluid, so gating on its validity would let a non-existent phase veto carbon balance);
     invalid speciation ⇒ return +∞, which is what lets the bisection reject that aC from above.

 3.4  THE SOLVE                                                    [_solve_aC:424]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
     ? nC_total = 0                    ⇒ aC = 0
     ? D(aC_cap) ≤ nC_total (finite)   ⇒ aC = aC_cap, SOLID CARBON PRESENT, holding
                                          nC_solid = max(0, nC_total - nC_melt - nC_fluid)  (3.3)
     ? otherwise                       ⇒ bisect D(aC) = nC_total over
                                          log₁₀aC ∈ [-300, log₁₀aC_cap], 120 iters      (3.4)
                                          and nC_solid = 0 — carbon-undersaturated.

   ! Floor -300, not -40: Bmelt can exceed 10⁴⁰ at high fO2, so a floor of 10⁻⁴⁰ leaves carbon
     over-committed AT the bracket edge and the bisection converges wrong rather than failing.
   ! Bisected in log₁₀aC because D spans decades. No lower sign check is needed — the branch is
     only reached when D(aC_cap) > nC_total, and D → 0 as aC → 0.

 3.5  THE φ ↔ aC OUTER LOOP                                          [coh_state:439]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   φ and aC are mutually dependent. Successive substitution, 6 passes, from the ideal gas φᵢ = 1:
       (a) aC ← _solve_aC(…, φ frozen)          inner bisection, pure arithmetic
       (b) speciate at (f, aC, φ)               (2.18)-(2.22)
       (c) x ← x/Σx ; φ ← ZD09(x, Pbar, T)      the ONE expensive call                (3.5)

   ! This nesting is what makes the model tractable: the inner bisection runs 120 iterations with
     φ FROZEN, so the EOS is called 6 times per trial f instead of 6×120.
   ! FIXED 6 passes, NO convergence test and no diagnostic if it stalls.
   ! WHAT φ SOLVES — a different problem from carbon balance. K1-K4 are written in FUGACITIES, so
     φ = 1 would solve every equilibrium as an IDEAL GAS at multi-GPa pressure. It does not cancel,
     because φ differs species by species (H₂O and CO₂ most). This loop handles REAL-FLUID
     NON-IDEALITY; _solve_aC handles carbon balance at whatever φ the previous pass supplied.
   ! HOW φ IS QUANTIFIED — by definition, not a separate fit. φᵢ ≡ fᵢ/(xᵢ·Pbar), and zd_fugacity
     already builds fug[i] = Pbar·exp(lnγᵢ)·vx[i], so
         newφ = fug[i]/(vx[i]·Pbar) = exp(lnγᵢ)                                       (3.6)
     exactly — vx[i] and Pbar cancel algebraically, verified bit-for-bit down to vx[i]=10⁻⁹. All
     the physics is in lnγᵢ (4.5). The loop's only real decision is whether to KEEP it: for a
     trace species the ratios εᵢ/ε, σᵢ/σ can lose precision before that cancellation completes,
     so a non-finite or non-positive value is rejected in favour of the previous pass.

===============================================================================================
 PART 4 — THERMODYNAMIC PRIMITIVES
===============================================================================================

 4.1  GIBBS ENERGY AT TEMPERATURE — Holland & Powell (1998)        [hp98_gibbs:245]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   With Cp = c1 + c2·T + c3·T⁻² + c5·T⁻⁰·⁵ and Tr = 298.15:
       ΔH = c1(T-Tr) + ½c2(T²-Tr²) - c3(1/T - 1/Tr) + 2c5(√T - √Tr)                  (4.1)
       ΔS = c1·ln(T/Tr) + c2(T-Tr) - ½c3(1/T² - 1/Tr²) - 2c5(1/√T - 1/√Tr)           (4.2)
       G(T) = G0 - (T-Tr)·S + ΔH - T·ΔS                                              (4.3)

 4.2  PRESSURE TERM FOR SOLIDS — modified Tait/Murnaghan          [murnaghan_dG:253]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       V0(T) = V0·exp[ b1(T-Tr) + 2b5(√T - √Tr) ] ;   K = b6 + b7(T-Tr) ;   K' = b8
       ΔG_P  = V0(T)·K/(K'-1) · [ (1 + K'(Pbar-1)/K)^(1-1/K') - 1 ]                  (4.4)

 4.3  EQUILIBRIUM CONSTANTS                                       [reaction_Ks:262]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       μC = min[ G_graphite(T,P), G_diamond(T,P) ]                                   (4.5)
       K1 = exp[-(μCO2 - μC  - μO2 )/(R·T)]      K2 = exp[-(μCO  - μC  - ½μO2)/(R·T)]
       K3 = exp[-(μH2O - μH2 - ½μO2)/(R·T)]      K4 = exp[-(μCH4 - μC  - 2μH2)/(R·T)]  (4.6)

   ! Only the carbon polymorphs carry (4.4); gases are referenced at 1 bar, which is what makes
     fO₂ and the φᵢ·P products the pressure-carrying quantities.
   ! Using graphite past the diamond transition (~2-5 GPa) would exaggerate its pressure-driven
     destabilisation — hence the min in (4.5).

 4.4  THE EMOG(D) BOUNDARY                                       [logfO2_EMOG:517]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       2 MgCO₃ + Mg₂Si₂O₆ = 2 Mg₂SiO₄ + 2C + 2O₂                                     (4.7)
       ΔG = 2G(Fo) + 2μC + 2G(O₂) - 2G(Mag) - G(En) ;   fE = -ΔG/(2·R·T·ln10)        (4.8)
     f < fE ⇒ graphite/diamond is the stable solid carbon host;  f > fE ⇒ carbonate is.

 4.5  THE C-O-H EQUATION OF STATE — Zhang & Duan (2009)     [zd_volume:328, zd_fugacity:343]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   PRINCIPLE: CORRESPONDING STATES. One universal EOS (4.11) in REDUCED variables, with species
   differing only through Lennard-Jones ε [K] and σ [Å]. Two fluids at the same (Tm,Pm) share Vm.

       ZD_eps = (154.0, 510.0, 235.0, 31.2, 105.6, 124.5, 246.1)   order:
       ZD_sig = (3.691, 2.88, 3.79, 2.93, 3.66, 3.36, 4.35)        (CH₄,H₂O,CO₂,H₂,CO,O₂,C₂H₆)

   ! METHANE IS THE REFERENCE FLUID: the constants 154 and 3.06258806161 are chosen so that for
     PURE CH₄ the reduced variables equal the real ones (checked: Tm/T = 1.0, Pm/P = 0.999999999999).

   MIXING, FIRST ENTRY — van der Waals ONE-FLUID theory: the mixture is collapsed into a single
   PSEUDO-PURE fluid by mole-fraction-averaging (ε,σ) over every pair (geometric mean for energy,
   arithmetic for size — Lorentz-Berthelot), corrected by binary parameters K1ᵢⱼ, K2ᵢⱼ:
       ε = Σᵢ Σⱼ xᵢxⱼ·K1ᵢⱼ·√(εᵢεⱼ)     K1[H2O,CO2]=0.85, K1[CH4,H2O]=0.8, else 1      (4.9)
       σ = Σᵢ Σⱼ xᵢxⱼ·K2ᵢⱼ·(σᵢ+σⱼ)/2    K2[H2O,CO2]=1.02,                 else 1
   ! Non-unity only for H₂O pairs: its polarity needs an empirical correction the nonpolar-
     calibrated framework lacks. ONE EOS solve for the whole mixture — that is the "one-fluid".

       Pm = 3.06258806161·σ³·Pbar/ε          Tm = 154·T/ε                            (4.10)
       Z = 1 + (c1 + c2/Tm² + c3/Tm³)/V + (c4 + c5/Tm² + c6/Tm³)/V²
             + (c7 + c8/Tm² + c9/Tm³)/V⁴ + (c10 + c11/Tm² + c12/Tm³)/V⁵
             + (c13/Tm³/V²)·(c14 + c15/V²)·exp(-c15/V²)                              (4.11)
       solve  R_gas·Tm·Z/V = Pm  for V ;   V[cc/mol] = 1000·V·(σ/3.691)³             (4.12)

   ! (4.11) is a Benedict-Webb-Rubin virial expansion in 1/V with T-dependent coefficients, plus
     one Duan-lineage exponential attraction term a pure power series cannot capture cleanly from
     dilute gas to dense supercritical fluid. All 15 constants are UNIVERSAL; only (ε,σ) vary.

   FUGACITY COEFFICIENTS — MIXING, SECOND ENTRY. φᵢ = fᵢ/(xᵢ·Pbar) is the standard departure
   integral, and because P depends on composition only through ε(x), σ(x), the chain rule runs it
   through the SAME mixing rule, producing per-species ONE-SIDED partial sums:
       εᵢ = Σⱼ K1ᵢⱼ·xⱼ·√(εᵢεⱼ)          σᵢ = Σⱼ K2ᵢⱼ·xⱼ·(σᵢ+σⱼ)/2                    (4.12a)
       ln γᵢ = Z - 1 - ln Z + S1 - 2·S2·(1 - εᵢ/ε) + 6(1-Z)·(1 - σᵢ/σ)               (4.13)
       fᵢ = xᵢ·Pbar·exp(ln γᵢ)   ⇒   φᵢ = exp(ln γᵢ)   — see (3.6)

   ! S1, S2 are the volume integral of (4.11) and its Tm-derivative moment. Their STRUCTURAL
     correspondence to (4.11) was verified directly (S1 reuses its terms with 1,½,¼,⅕ against
     V⁻¹,V⁻²,V⁻⁴,V⁻⁵; S2 reuses S1 with the T-power multiplied through, e.g. 2c2,3c3), but the
     closed-form derivation is inherited from the Lee-Kesler-Plöcker lineage, not re-derived here.
   ! PHYSICAL READING of (4.13): (Z-1)-lnZ is the whole-mixture ideal-gas departure; S1 the
     pure-EOS pressure integral; -2S2(1-εᵢ/ε) corrects for species i's own ε differing from the
     mixture average (Tm ∝ 1/ε); 6(1-Z)(1-σᵢ/σ) the size analogue (Vm ∝ 1/σ³ — the 6 is 2×3).
   ! (4.12) has several real roots. Brackets tried widest-first — (1,1000), (0.01,10), (0.001,1) —
     to land on the fluid-like branch. A heuristic, not Gibbs-energy phase selection.
   ! 3.06258806161 is ε(CH₄)/σ(CH₄)³ at full precision, not the paper's rounded 3.0636.
   ! C₂H₆ is carried in the arrays but never populated: its mass-action term scales as P²,
     amplifying any inconsistency between an external ethane dataset and HP98.
   ! Calibrated 673-2573 K and ≤10 GPa; outside that the speciation is extrapolated and the driver
     says so.

 4.6  REFERENCE BUFFERS — REPORTING ONLY, never entering the solve   [Section 4c]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   BOTH out of sb24, the dataset that produced the fO2 they are subtracted from. An offset means
   nothing unless buffer and fO2 share a dataset.
       FMQ:  3 fa + O₂ = 3 qtz + 2 mag      IW:  4 Fe + 2 O₂ = Fe₄O₄                 (4.14)
       log₁₀fO2 = (ΔG - G°(O₂))/(R·T·ln10),  ΔG = oxidised side - reduced side per mole O₂

   G of each end-member is computed HERE from SLB (2024) Table C1 — Mie-Grüneisen-Debye on a BM3
   cold curve, Newton on V, plus metal electronic (A2-A3) and bcc-Fe magnetic (A5-A9) terms — so
   the buffers need no live MAGEMin handle. FMQ prefers MAGEMin's own fO2 - dQFM when a solid scan
   ran; the Gibbs form agrees to <0.01 log units.
   ! SLB's `wu` is Fe₄O₄, not FeO (`pe` likewise Mg₄O₄), so IW is written per TWO O₂.
   ! sb24's IW sits ~1.4 log units high because MAGEMin never applies the Landau term, so every
     ferrous host is short R·ln5 per Fe²⁺. It CANCELS in ΔIW — do not add it to IW alone.
   ! Frost (1991) forms are retained as logfO2_*_frost for the benchmark scripts only. Subtracting
     them from an sb24 fO2 is a category error; see TOex_bench.txt §1.7-1.8.

 4.7  SMOOTHING AND GUARD FUNCTIONS
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       softmax2(a,b,β) = m + ln[e^{β(a-m)} + e^{β(b-m)}]/β,   m = max(a,b)
       softmin2(a,b,β) = -softmax2(-a,-b,β)                                          (4.15)
       softcap(x,cap)  = max(0, softmin2(x, cap, 30/cap))

   ! Caps from ABOVE only: a smooth lower rounding would inject a phantom floor into XCO₂, which
     Φ then amplifies into a spurious oxygen demand. max(0,·) trims the few-ulp undershoot at x≈0.

===============================================================================================
 PART 5 — THE EXACT PROCESS, STEP BY STEP
===============================================================================================
 Steps 2-4 are the residual, evaluated ~40-60 times.

 STEP 0 — SETUP, once per call                                    [partition_Oₑₓ:833]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   0.1  X ← p·XB + (1-p)·XH                                                    (0.1)
   0.2  Masses Mf, Ms, Mc, Mh, TOₑₓ; moles molMf, nC_total; the factor Φ       (0.2)-(0.6)
   0.3  ? TOₑₓ = 0  ⇒  RETURN immediately, nothing to partition
   0.4  IDV ← ∫ΔV dP, for the melt oxybarometer
   0.5  Ceilings maxsOₑₓ (2.2), maxmOₑₓ (2.9); lowclip = 10⁻¹⁰
   0.6  BUILD THE SOLID OXYBAROMETER — the expensive step, nr+nrm MAGEMin calls  (2.1)-(2.6)
        Returns sfO2, table bounds, monotonicity flag, the metal floor (2.2b), QFM, and the
        native-iron interpolant.   ? Ms = 0 (ϕ=1) ⇒ skip MAGEMin, return a placeholder
   0.7  minsOₑₓ ← -s_floor;  pack ctx

 STEP 1 — BRACKET                                                   [bisect_solve:807]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   1.1  Evaluate F(-120) and F(+40).
   1.2  ? sign F(-120) = sign F(+40) ⇒ NO ROOT. Return the endpoint with smaller |F|, flagged
        converged = false. This is the `capacity_exceeded` exit.
   1.3  Otherwise the root is bracketed.
   ! The bracket is far wider than any physical fO2. Every demand saturates outside its own range,
     so this only has to straddle the root — it is not a constraint on the answer.

 STEP 2 — AT A TRIAL f: THE TWO Fe SINKS                          [_coh_derive:737]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Each inverts a monotone oxybarometer, 80 bisections, saturation branches checked FIRST:
     2.1  SOLID over [-minsOₑₓ + lowclip, maxsOₑₓ - lowclip] — the bracket STRADDLES ZERO:
          ? hi ≤ lo         ⇒ sOₑₓ = lo.  Zero capacity — ABSENT (ϕ=1).
          ? sfO2(lo) ≥ f    ⇒ sOₑₓ = lo.  PINNED AT THE METAL FLOOR: all Fe as Fe⁰, not buffering.
          ? sfO2(hi) ≤ f    ⇒ sOₑₓ = hi.  PINNED AT CAP: every Fe²⁺ oxidised, no longer buffering.
          ? otherwise       ⇒ bisect for sfO2(sOₑₓ) = f exactly.
     2.2  MELT: identical, over [lowclip, maxmOₑₓ - lowclip] — no metal branch.
   ! Only in the "otherwise" branch does that sink's own fO2 equal f. In the pinned branches it
     does not, and that is correct, not an error (1.2). classify_regime records which.

 STEP 3 — AT THE SAME f: THE JOINT CARBON + FLUID SOLVE            [coh_state:439]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   3.1  Pbar ← 10⁴P;  fO₂ ← 10^f;  K1..K4 ← (4.6);  molH ← (2.23);  nC_total ← (0.5)
   3.2  Bmelt ← (2.11);  fE ← (4.8);  aC_cap ← min(1, 10^(fE - f))                   (3.1)
   3.3  Initialise φᵢ = 1 (ideal gas), aC = aC_cap
   3.4  REPEAT 6 TIMES:                                                              (3.5)
        (a) aC with φ frozen [_solve_aC]:
            ? nC_total = 0          ⇒ aC = 0
            ? D(aC_cap) ≤ nC_total  ⇒ aC = aC_cap ⇒ SOLID CARBON PRESENT, holding (3.3)
            ? otherwise (or D = ∞)  ⇒ bisect (3.4) ⇒ NO SOLID CARBON, carbon-undersaturated.
                                      This branch also resolves an INVALID speciation, since
                                      D = +∞ there pushes aC down.
        (b) speciate at (f, aC, φ): (2.18)-(2.22). Σx = 1 by construction.
        (c) x ← x/Σx; φᵢ ← ZD09 (4.9)-(4.13). Non-finite or non-positive values discarded.
   3.5  MELT CO₂ (2.12)-(2.13);  FLUID (2.24)-(2.25)
   3.6  SOLID CARBON: present only if aC is still pinned at its ceiling —
            solid_present = (nC_total > 0) AND (aC ≥ aC_cap·(1 - 10⁻⁹))
   3.7  HOST SPLIT (2.15)-(2.17);  FLUID OXYGEN (2.26)-(2.27)
   ! CARBON MASS BALANCE HOLDS EXACTLY AT EVERY TRIAL f, not only at the root. Step 3 is a complete
     carbon solve nested inside each oxygen residual; the outer bisection solves OXYGEN alone.

 STEP 4 — ASSEMBLE THE RESIDUAL                                       [_coh_derive:756]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   4.1  carbonO ← Φ·XCO₂/(1 - XCO₂)                                                  (2.14)
   4.2  Check fₑₓ, carbO, fₛ, fₗ are finite; abort loudly if not [nancheck]
   4.3  Record each phase's OWN reading: fₛ = sfO2(sOₑₓ), fₗ = melt_fO2(mOₑₓ)
   4.4  total ← sOₑₓ + mOₑₓ + carbonO + carbO + fₑₓ ;   F(f) ← total - 1             (1.1)

 STEP 5 — BISECTION UPDATE                                          [bisect_solve:816]
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   5.1  x ← ½(lo + hi);  fx ← F(x)
   5.2  ? |fx| ≤ 10⁻⁸  OR  (hi - lo) ≤ 10⁻¹⁴·max(1,|x|)   ⇒ stop
   5.3  ? sign fx = sign F(lo)  ⇒ lo ← x   else   hi ← x ;   repeat from STEP 2, ≤500 iters
   ! CONVERGENCE IS JUDGED ON THE RESIDUAL ALONE. A collapsed bracket with |F| still large means
     F is DISCONTINUOUS there and no root exists — which must not be reported as success.

 STEP 6 — POST-PROCESS
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   6.1  Re-derive the full state at the converged f, so what is reported is exactly what the
        residual saw — the same function, not a reconstruction.
   6.2  Iron speciation (2.6a)-(2.6b): metal_wt, FeM_solid/net/diss/disp, FeR_solid/net, FeR_melt.
   6.3  Reference buffers (4.14); classify_regime — a label only, never an input to the solve.
   6.4  Return both budgets as fractions of their own totals:
          sOₑₓ, mOₑₓ, carbonO, carbO, cohsink — of TOₑₓ, summing to 1 when converged
          fC_gph, fC_carb, fC_melt, fC_fluid  — of TC,   summing to 1 when TC > 0

===============================================================================================
 PART 6 — THE LIMITS, AND WHAT HAPPENS WHEN EACH IS TOUCHED
===============================================================================================
 A limit being touched is a branch, not an error. Only the last row is a failure.
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LIMIT                    TEST                        CONSEQUENCE
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  solid at metal floor     sfO2(-minsOₑₓ) ≥ f          sOₑₓ = -minsOₑₓ. All Fe as Fe⁰; the solid
                                                       has left the equilibrium, so fₛ ≠ f is
                                                       legitimate.  :solid_metal_saturated

  solid at cap             sfO2(maxsOₑₓ) ≤ f           Every Fe²⁺ oxidised, sink full.
                                                       :solid_saturated

  melt at floor / cap      same with melt_fO2          :melt_depleted / :melt_saturated

  both Fe caps             both saturated              :oxidant_exhausted — the rock's capacity
                                                       binds the whole budget

  phase absent             maxsOₑₓ ≤ 0 or maxmOₑₓ ≤ 0  ABSENT, not merely empty: no oxybarometer
                                                       exists, so any fO2 attributed to it is
                                                       fictitious and suppressed in the report.
                                                       :melt_only / :solid_only

  melt CO₂ ceiling         aC·Bmelt → XCO2max = 0.3    softcap rolls XCO₂ over smoothly, stopping
                                                       (2.13) diverging near the carbonate boundary

  carbon over-committed    D(aC_cap) > nC_total        aC drops below its ceiling (3.4) until melt
                                                       + fluid hold exactly nC_total; solid carbon
                                                       disappears.  :carbon_undersaturated — the
                                                       normal resolution

  fluid overfilled         γ ≤ 0 or disc < 0 (2.20)    speciation invalid ⇒ D = +∞ ⇒ the same aC
                                                       reduction resolves it. Not a failure.

  EMOG(D) crossed          f ≈ fE                      solid carbon splits over a 0.25-log ramp
                                                       (2.15).  :carbonate_graphite_buffered

  MAGEMin table edge       sOₑₓ ∉ [tbl_lo, tbl_hi]     below: held FLAT at the metal plateau;
                                                       above: log-linear (2.6). Answer valid, but
                                                       the report says it relies on extrapolation.

  MAGEMin non-monotonic    !issorted(fO2)              repaired by isotonic regression (2.3)-(2.4)
                                                       and warned. Without it the root can vanish.

  metal vs budget clash    FeM_net > FeM_solid         sOₑₓ implies more metal than the assemblage
                                                       holds — the R axis is too coarse near the
                                                       metal edge. Flagged, never absorbed.

  NO ROOT AT ALL           sign F(-120) = sign F(+40)  :capacity_exceeded. Even with every sink at
                                                       its cap the assemblage cannot hold the
                                                       budget — physically, a new Fe³⁺-rich phase
                                                       must precipitate. Reported, never patched.
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 ! The shortfall 1 - Σ is printed WITH ITS SIGN, because the directions mean opposite things:
     > 0  UNPLACED — that much of TOₑₓ has no sink;  < 0  OVER-SUBSCRIBED — the sinks demand more.
 ! In testing every such case was pinned by the sampled Fe³⁺ range (Rmax), not by stoichiometry.
   Raise Rmax before concluding a new phase is needed.

===============================================================================================
 PART 8 — WHAT THE MATHEMATICS DOES NOT CAPTURE
===============================================================================================
 Each is a property of the FRAMEWORK, not of the implementation.

   1. ABSOLUTE fO2 OFFSET, ~1.9 log units. Relative behaviour benchmarks well; the mapping onto an
      absolute scale is shifted. ~1.4 of it is now attributed to MAGEMin's missing Landau term
      (bench §1.8, issue 10). The reference-buffer half of the original diagnosis is resolved.

   2. NO PRECIPITATING Fe³⁺ PHASE. The five sinks are the only sinks; a budget exceeding their
      combined capacity is reported, not resolved.

   3. XCO2max = 0.3 stands in for a real CO₂ solubility model in silicate melt.

   4. Carbonate is pure MgCO₃ at unit activity, not a magnesite-dolomite-calcite solution; the
      wEMOG ramp approximates the resulting smearing rather than modelling it.

   5. The φ ↔ aC loop is a FIXED 6 passes with no convergence test.

   6. C₂H₆ is excluded from the fluid.

   7. METAL CANNOT DRIVE THE SOLVE. Disproportionation is oxygen-neutral, so it is the null
      direction of sOₑₓ; metal is harvested from the assemblage and REPORTED (2.6a), never solved
      for. Driving it needs Fe³⁺ and Fe⁰ as independent unknowns — a second equation.

   8. No kinetics anywhere: every equation above is an equilibrium statement at a single (P,T),
      and all sinks are assumed to equilibrate instantaneously.
