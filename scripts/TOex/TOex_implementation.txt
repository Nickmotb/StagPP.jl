           where it is INERT - no T, no P, no phase, reachable only by a later ingassing step.
           Physically the un-subducted part is OFFSCRAPED / UNDERPLATED / left on the overriding
           plate, which is a property of the TRENCH and the incoming plate. The code decides it
           from the wt% ceiling of the RECEIVING MANTLE TRACER, which is none of those things.
           ! AND THE MAGNITUDE SAYS THE CEILING IS LOAD-BEARING FOR THE WRONG REASON. avCrbxCell
             is ~2E+17 kg per cell and the rate law offers max(vn*dt/L_ing) ~ 0.44 of it PER STEP

             enormous and w_max_C is the only brake on it, so what gets rejected is the gap
             between an unphysical offer and what the destination can hold. NEITHER number is a
             subduction flux, which is why the rejected mass has no physical reading today.
           THE FIX IS SOURCE-SIDE, one of: an explicit offscraping/accretion fraction at the
           trench, or a rate law calibrated to a real subducted-carbon flux instead of a fraction
           of the cell's stock. Then w_max_C goes back to being a validity backstop and the
           rejected mass becomes accreted carbonate, which is a thing rather than a residue.

 A.1  THE THREE LEVELS   - each quantity lives at exactly one
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   TRACER   every conserved quantity, and H2O solubility.
            H2O, H2Ofree, OxC, ReC, OEX, CFLUID, OFLUID, WSOL, fO2, F3FT, SCRB
   CELL     the TOex solve and nothing else. Inputs summed from the cell's tracers AFTER
            solubility; outputs written back onto them.
            ? WHY NOT PER TRACER: a tracer is either solid or molten, so it could only ever pass
              phi = 0 or 1 - the degenerate branches where one Fe3+ host is ABSENT, and the
              solid<->melt Fe3+ exchange would not exist. The cell is the smallest volume in which
              the coexistence being solved for is represented.
   GRID     derived intensive state, cached for the step, never advected: fO2, the five COH mole
            fractions, rho_fluid, eta_fluid. Diagnostics and coefficients only.

 A.2  WHERE THE SOLVE SITS IN THE TIMESTEP    (inside TraMeltErupt)
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       1  flow solve
       2  carbon ingassing            (new velocities, before advection)
       3  advect tracers
       4  melting / eruption          (solidus reads C(c_oxC), lagged one step - B.9)
       5  SOLID solubility            S_eff = S*a_H2O^n via B.16's IMPLICIT update       3.C.1
       6  gather -> CELL TOex solve   fO2, sinks, speciation, AND the melt's hydrogen  3.B.1 3.C
       7  scatter back to tracers     incremental for rock, proportional for fluid     3.B.2 3.D
       8  outgassing                  speciated, from 6                                3.F.1
       9  COH redistribution / Darcy  on the (H,C,O_ex) triple, rho and eta from 6     3.E
      10  tracer -> grid interpolation, for output and for step n+1's solidus
   ! No grid->tracer interpolation of speciation is needed: rho and eta are consumed as cell
     scalars in the same cell the solve ran in.
   ! TWO LAGS REMAIN, both one step: a_H2O into 5 (B.16) and the carbonate fraction into 4's
     solidus (B.9). Each output is produced by 6, downstream of its consumer.
   ! THE MELT'S HYDROGEN IS NOT ONE OF THEM. It moved into 6 (B.19) because its capacity needs
     f_H2, for which no lagged proxy exists. Step 5 handles the SOLID only, and its lag is safe
     only because of B.16's implicit update - explicitly lagged it is unstable at G ~ 1e2.
   ! Fluid arriving in a new cell is not re-speciated until the next solve unless 3.E's
     react-in-transit / parcel-refresh flags are on. Conservation is safe either way because
     OFLUID is TRANSPORTED explicitly, never recomputed on arrival.

 A.3  RUNTIME ALGORITHM, TABLED MAGEMIN
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   ? A FULL LOOKUP OF THE TOex OUTPUTS WAS REJECTED: the input space is 7-D (1.3e9 nodes at 20 per
     dimension) and the regime boundaries are near-discontinuities, so interpolating across one
     returns shares that do not sum to 1 - and renormalising breaks the shared-fO2 construction.
   EXPENSIVE AND TABULATABLE: MAGEMin, which depends only on (P,T,p,R). Tabled - see A.4.
   NOT TABULATABLE AND NOT CHEAP: the solve itself. As first ported, 1.14 ms/cell (34 outer
   bisections from the full bracket) = 18.7 s/step on 16384 cells, 2.3x the whole timestep.
   The three levers, as originally reasoned, and what they actually turned out to be worth:
     (1) TOLERANCE - exiting at the table's own ~0.01 resolution rather than 1e-9. Superseded in
         practice by 3.O's bit-exact bracket exit, which was bigger AND changed no output.
     (2) WARM START - bisection is LOGARITHMIC in bracket width, so narrowing 25 -> 4 log units
         buys 2.6 halvings, not 20. Free, but never the lever intuition says it is.
     (3) DIRTY-FLAG SKIPPING - linear in the fraction skipped, so the only lever that can buy an
         order of magnitude, and the only one that changes the answer. Built as 3.E.8; measured
         2.3x on the solve but only 4 % of the run, and it moves dFMQ - left OFF (bench 8).
   WHAT ACTUALLY PAID: 3.O's zd_bisect exit, the aC hoist and Illinois - 1.68x on the solve with
   72/72 files byte-identical for the first two. TOex is 44 % of runtime, so 1.79x is the ceiling
   on ALL TOex work.

 A.4  THE SOLID TABLES: AXIS, RESOLUTION, FILE CONTRACT
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   THE AXIS IS THE WHOLE PROBLEM. The oxybarometer is log-linear in Fe3+/Fe2+ = R/(1-R), so
   sampling uniformly in R fights a logarithm. Max |error| in log10 fO2, R in [1e-3, 0.185]:
         nR      uniform-in-R    log-spaced
         17          1.67           0.032
         49          0.68           0.0036
         65          0.51           0.0020
   UNIFORM CANNOT REACH THE TARGET AT ANY PRACTICAL NODE COUNT. No byte budget buys past a wrong axis.
   ! THE R CEILING IS MAGEMin'S, NOT A CHOICE. sb24 stops returning finite fO2 above the sampled
     top (beyond R = 0.4 here). CONSEQUENCE: bridgmanite wants Fe3+/SumFe ~ 0.5-0.7, above
     anything tabulated, so lower-mantle fO2 rests on the log-linear EXTRAPOLATION - the same form
     TOex.jl falls back on, so the two stay consistent, but GATE 1 must TEST that, not assume it.
   THE TABLE PAIR AS OF 2026-09-03 - generated together, cross-validated at load:
       StagfO2.dat    100 x 100 x 30, R in [-1.96, 0.40], uniform in asinh(R/2e-3) to 2.7e-15
       StagMetal.dat  identical grid and map order, native Fe as a WEIGHT FRACTION of the solid
     (P,T) 100x100 per sector sits near 0.008 RMS; finer buys little because the MAX error is set
     by phase-assemblage discontinuities and does not converge. ~26 MB, ~2.94e6 minimizations.
     load_s_fO2_data ABORTS if the metal grid or R axis disagrees - they would describe different
     rocks. A MISSING metal table is not fatal; the solver degrades to FeM_net.
     ! THE TWO ALWAYS REGENERATE TOGETHER: same minimisations, so a mismatched pair is not a
       partial update, it is two different rocks.
     ! 120 of 1.8M nodes non-finite - the SAME 2 (P,T) cells in the TZ sector at T ~ 3970-4000 K,
       every R, both endmembers. Fully molten, benign, filled from finite neighbours at load.
   FILE CONTRACT: the R axis is written EXPLICITLY as nR values on header line 5. Reconstructing
   it from min/max as an even grid silently mislocates every interior node.
   INTERPOLATION COORDINATE: u = asinh(R/R0) on the signed axis (the logit log(R/(1-R)) is
   undefined at R <= 0). Interpolating in R throws the axis away.
   ! Metal is CLAMPED in R at both ends where fO2 is not - there is no metal above the sampled
     range and no more of it below, so extrapolating either end invents iron.
   LOADER CONTRACT, three rules the multi-R path depends on:
     (a) thermochem_int's Hint means H2O for 'solubility' and R for 'fO2'. Clamp it to the
         solubility table's H2O axis ON THE SOLUBILITY PATH ONLY - clamping on the fO2 path pins R
         to the H2O axis, which has one node.
     (b) Non-finite nodes are repaired at load from finite neighbours, with a warning, and abort
         if a node has no finite neighbour. Untouched they poison the four cells around each.
     (c) Maps read are COUNTED, so a truncated file is a diagnosed abort, not whole maps undefined.
   ! GATE 1 TRAP. TOex.jl builds its own interpolant on a much finer axis than the table, so at
     low R THE FORTRAN IS MORE ACCURATE THAN THE REFERENCE and they will disagree by far more than
     0.01. Settle the comparison basis before GATE 1 runs, or it measures the wrong thing.

 PART B - DECISIONS TAKEN

 B.1  REFERENCE STATE
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Excess oxygen against Fe as FeO, C as graphite, H as H2O:
       CO2 / carbonate +2 per C · CO +1 · organic C, graphite 0 · CH4 -2 · H2O 0 · H2 -1 per H2
       Fe3+ +0.5 per Fe · Fe0 -1 per Fe
   Mass conversion: 2 O per C = 2*15.999/12.011 = 2.6642 kg O per kg C.
   ! EVERY entry and exit point uses this convention, through ONE helper (B.6). If it is
     duplicated, the budget drifts and the failure looks like a solver problem.
   These oxygen numbers are also what 3.N.3's COH redox index averages over.

 B.2  CARBON-OFF COMPATIBILITY IS A GATING GUARANTEE, NOT A PHYSICS IDENTITY
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   With traceelement_carbon = .false. the solver is never called, the tables are never loaded, and
   every new field sits inside `if (traceelement_carbon)`. So CFLUID == 0, OFLUID == 0, a_H2O == 1
   and the redistribution behaves exactly as today.
   ! A CARBON-FREE FLUID IS NOT A PURE-H2O FLUID, and its O_ex is not zero. Measured with Mc = 0
     at 3 GPa / 1500 K:
         log fO2   -18     -15     -12      -9      -6
         X_H2      0.999   0.980   0.504   0.008   0.0002
         fex      -8.88   -8.71   -4.47   -0.067  -0.002
     Water dissociates to H2 + 1/2 O2 at low fO2 with no carbon anywhere. THE IDENTITY HOLDS
     BECAUSE THE SOLVER IS BYPASSED, NOT BECAUSE THE CHEMISTRY IS TRIVIAL. Anything that runs the
     solver on a pure H-O system sees a large negative OFLUID, and any H2-solubility term (F.8) is
     live with carbon off and therefore needs its own switch, defaulting off.
   ! Protected by GATE 2a. The other place it is at risk is H_escape_mode - B.13. And B.20's
     'sunyao' default breaks it deliberately: every GATE 2a/3a run must set 'katz'.

 B.3  COMPOSITION BASE: EM_Xhz / EM_Xbs
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   defaults_include.f90 already holds the endmembers TOex uses (same 7 oxides, same order,
   reordered in refstate.f90), so X = p*EM_Xbs + (1-p)*EM_Xhz ports verbatim.
   ? FEOPOS IS NOT USED. Under tracers_FeNum it holds Fe# against a DIFFERENT oxide basis (4
     oxides, mole%, Xu et al. 2009). Mixing bases corrupts the Fe inventory, which is what
     converts O_ex <-> Fe3+. See F.1 for how to add it safely.

 B.4  TRACER FIELDS

 B.10-B.15  THE SURFACE RESERVOIR   (Phase 4, designed not built)
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   B.10  TWO BOXES, (C, O_ex) PAIRS.  A = atmosphere + ocean, receives degassing (C_A, O_A);
     B = ingassable crust/sediment, feeds ingassing (C_B, O_B).
     ? The currency is (C, O_ex), NOT an ox/red binary: a binary cannot represent CH4, which is a
       REDUCTANT (O_ex = -2), not merely unoxidised.
     DIAGNOSTICS Theta = O_A/C_A - 2 (>0 oxic with free O2, =0 exactly CO2, -2<.<0 CO2+CO/CH4,
     <=-2 net reducing) and O_B/C_B, which is what gets subducted.
   B.11  BURIAL A -> B IS TWO INDEPENDENT SCALARS.
     ! NEVER write burial as "move carbon at box A's prevailing O/C ratio" - written that way f_org
       does nothing and organic burial is meaningless.
         dC = burial carbon flux;  dO = 2*(1 - f_org)*dC*(M_O/M_C)
         C_B += dC, O_B += dO, C_A -= dC, O_A -= dO
     The residual 2*f_org*dC*(M_O/M_C) STAYS IN A by construction. THAT ONE ASYMMETRY IS THE ENTIRE
     REDOX ENGINE OF THE SURFACE SYSTEM - it is how organic burial produces free O2.
       RATE  F_bur = min( C_A*(1 - exp(-dt/tau_burial)), headroom(B) )   CAP  C_B <= (w_max_C/100)*M
     ! The exponential matters: StagYY steps (1e3-1e5 yr) are comparable to plausible tau_burial,
       so a linear C_A*dt/tau would overshoot.
     ! When B saturates, burial backs up and C_A rises - a high-CO2 greenhouse. A RESULT, not a
       clamp. Same constraint as the per-tracer ingassing cap (4.7); both use the same w_max_C.
   B.12  THE STEADY STATE. With r_deg = O_ex/C of the erupted parcel, box A gives f_org = 1 - r_deg/2:
         r_deg +2 (CO2) -> f_org 0 · +1.6 -> 0.2 (~modern Earth) · 0 (graphite) -> 1, the pump's
         maximum · r_deg < 0 (CH4-rich) -> f_org > 1, IMPOSSIBLE.
     ! The last row is a PREDICTION: a mantle reduced enough to degas net-reducing carbon cannot
       sustain an oxidising surface, and f_org <= 1 means you cannot tune out of it. For r_deg < 0
       the missing sink is hydrogen escape.
     ! STRUCTURAL WARNING - the loop through B and the mantle is POSITIVE (reduced mantle ->
       CH4-rich degassing -> reducing A -> more organic burial -> reduced B -> little oxidant
       ingassed -> mantle stays reduced), symmetric on the oxidised branch, so the coupled system
       is plausibly BISTABLE near r_deg = 0. Mitigating: mantle Fe3+ is a far larger oxygen
       reservoir than carbon, so loop gain may be small; and H escape is a one-way oxidising term
       that breaks the symmetry. Probed deliberately by 5.6.
   B.13  HYDROGEN ESCAPE is one line, because the reference puts H in H2O:
       EVERY 2 H ATOMS THAT ESCAPE LEAVE +1 O OF EXCESS OXYGEN BEHIND, REGARDLESS OF CARRIER.
       H2O degassed dC 0, dO_ex 0 ... its 2 H escape dO_ex +1
       CH4 degassed dC +1, dO_ex -2 ... its 4 H escape dO_ex +2   NET dC +1, dO_ex 0
     ! So methane degassing plus complete escape leaves carbon at GRAPHITE equivalence, not CO2.
     ? PHYSICS: H2O is cold-trapped on a wet planet and barely escapes; CH4 and H2 reach the
       homopause. That asymmetry is why a CH4-rich Archean atmosphere is thought to have driven net
       surface oxidation.
     ? H_escape_mode is REQUIRED, no silent default: 'legacy' (water_budget(2) driven by
       outgas_fraction_water, exactly as today - protects B.2) or 'speciated' (H2O -> ocean,
       CH4/H2 -> escape, +1 O per 2 H credited to A). Today outgas_fraction_water = 1.0 sends 100 %
       of erupting tracers' water to space.
     ! PAYOFF: escape credits O to A, raises Theta, and once f_org responds (F.2) lowers organic
       burial - a NEGATIVE feedback, the term missing from B.12's loop. Plausibly what stabilises
       the system.
   B.14  SURFACE MODE. 'twobox' = internal model live, burial active. 'prescribed' = C_B and O_B/C_B
     held at namelist values, burial = 0, A a pure diagnostic - the hook for an external climate
     model and the control run 5.6 needs. ! Degassing speciation is computed in BOTH modes.
   B.15  f_org IS A CONSTANT for now. ! Unless it equals 1 - r_deg/2, box A drifts monotonically, so
     Theta must be in the time series. Bounded formulations are in F.2.

 B.16 WATER ACTIVITY IS A COUPLED EQUILIBRIUM, NOT A LOOKUP        (rewritten 2026-09-04)
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   THE PROBLEM. Storage capacity S is saturation against PURE water. Once the coexisting fluid is
   COH with X_H2O < 1 the true storage is lower, and with a reduced fluid MORE water should
   exsolve, not less.
         S_eff = S * a_H2O^n,   a_H2O = (phi_H2O/phi_H2O_pure)*X_H2O  ~  X_H2O
   ! Capping the transferable MASS instead was REJECTED: it leaves the equilibrium target at S, so
     an undersaturated tracer still walks to the wrong endpoint, just more slowly.
   ! WHY THE FORM IS RIGHT. NAM incorporation is a defect equilibrium in FUGACITY,
     c = A*f_H2O^n*exp(-dH/RT). Divide by the saturated value at the same P,T and the prefactors
     cancel: c/c_sat = a_H2O^n. It needs only that the reference is pure H2O at the same P,T, which
     holds for a buffered minimisation AND for a water-saturated experiment.
   ! ALL THREE SECTORS TAKE THE SAME SCALING, for different reasons. The UM is a_H2O = 1 by
     construction. The MTZ and LM get NO solubility from MAGEMin at all (sf_H2O.jl passes s=false
     and initialises sb24 bare), so they carry an observational parameterisation - and experimental
     storage capacities for wadsleyite, ringwoodite and bridgmanite are measured WATER-SATURATED,
     which IS a_H2O = 1. Same reference state either way.
     ? AND THE SCALING IS CLEANER THERE, NOT DIRTIER: in the UM a_H2O also controls the ASSEMBLAGE,
       so scaling S without re-minimising approximates a mineralogy change; stx24 is anhydrous and
       has no water response at all, so scaling the capacity IS the entire water feedback.
     ? n SHOULD BE PER SECTOR - olivine ~1, wadsleyite/ringwoodite < 1 (they flatten toward a
       stoichiometric limit), bridgmanite near 1. Three sectors already exist. Supersedes G.5.
   ! THE EXPLICIT LAG IS UNSTABLE, WHICH IS WHY THIS SECTION WAS REWRITTEN. a_H2O is a SHARED
     intensive variable; equilibrium is where a_rock = a_fluid. The lagged map linearises to
         W_{k+1} = S*a(W_k)^n,  a = f/(f+N),  f = H_tot - W
         G = |dW_{k+1}/dW_k| = n * W*N / ( f*(f+N) )
     UNSTABLE when W*N > f*(f+N), which is THE NORMAL MANTLE: 0.1 wt% bound, 0.001 wt% free H2O,
     0.01 wt% other fluid gives G ~ 91. The failure is a limit cycle - absorb, activity falls,
     expel, activity rises, absorb.
     ! REJECTED FIXES, so they are not revisited: a FLOOR on a_H2O invents a number and suppresses
       the legitimate case (a genuinely CH4-saturated rock SHOULD exsolve); UNDER-RELAXATION needs
       factor < 1/G ~ 0.01, hundreds of iterations; a TWO-REGIME SWITCH on "does a free fluid
       exist" with a_H2O = (W/S)^(1/n) when it does not - the branches agree ONLY at equilibrium,
       which a dynamic model does not have, so it is a cliff exactly where the gain is worst.
   ? DECIDED: AN IMPLICIT UPDATE USING THE ANALYTIC GAIN.
         W_new = W_old - (W_old - S*a_old^n) / (1 + G)
     Stable for ANY G, one extra expression, geometric convergence to the true coupled fixed point.
     a_H2O = 1 before the first solve, so behaviour is unchanged until then.
   ! G IS NOT A NUMERICAL NUISANCE, IT IS THE BUFFER RATIO. 1/(1+G) is the fraction of the
     disequilibrium relievable before the fluid's activity catches up. A rock buffering a trace
     fluid SHOULD barely move per step - THE DAMPING IS THE PHYSICS.
   ! DIAGNOSTIC, cheap and mandatory (5.4): per-cell a_H2O amplitude between consecutive steps.
     Ringing means the update is failing, and silently oscillating storage is invisible otherwise.
   ? BOTH SOLID AND MELT take the scaling, but only the SOLID this way - the melt moves inside the
     solve (B.19), and under B.20's 'sunyao' its activity enters through P_H2O instead.
   ! Carbon-off is inert by B.2. ! The rigorous alternative is an a_H2O axis on StagH2O.dat, exactly
     as fO2 got its R axis - deferred; the cheap version should first show that it matters.

 B.18 THE FLUID MOVES AS AN ELEMENT TRIPLE, NEVER AS A REDOX LABEL   (settled 2026-09-04)
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   THE PROBLEM. Free fluid is the one volatile reservoir that moves by something other than
   advection: WaterSaturationAndVerticalRedistribution marches a scalar in kg up each column,
   handing it between cells and subdomains. A parcel leaving a reduced cell and arriving in an
   oxidised one must deliver the right C and O, not just H.
   ? "OXIDISED / NEUTRAL / REDUCED" IS NOT TRANSPORTABLE. The same fluid is oxidising in one cell
     and reducing in another - the label is a RELATION between fluid and host, not a property of
     the fluid. What IS transportable is the element budget, and B.4 already provides it:
         (H2Ofree, CFLUID, OFLUID) = (H as H2O-equivalent, C, excess O signed)
     Three extensive amounts, complete, and MIXING IS EXACT ELEMENT-WISE ADDITION - no mixing rule
     for fO2, no averaging of an intensive variable. The receiving cell does not need to know where
     the parcel came from, only the three numbers.
   THE VALIDITY WINDOW, in mol:        -n_H/2  <=  n_Oex  <=  +2*n_C
     Upper bound: all C as CO2, and H cannot oxidise past H2O. Lower bound: NO carbon term, because
     CH4 (-2 per C over 4 H) and H2 (-1 per H2) are both exactly -0.5 per H - the most reducing
     thing available to a fluid is a function of its hydrogen alone. A parcel outside the window
     cannot exist; position INSIDE it is the redox state, computable with no thermodynamics.
     ! BOTH BOUNDS ARE LINEAR AND HOMOGENEOUS, SO THE WINDOW IS CLOSED UNDER POSITIVE SUMMATION: a
       sum of in-window parcels is in-window, and a violating sum REQUIRES A VIOLATING TERM. That
       is what makes 3.E.5's assertion able to localise a fault instead of merely reporting one.
   ? TWO TRANSPORT RULES, AND THEY DIFFER:
     DISSOLUTION  pool -> bound water.  H ONLY. It takes zero C and - because H2O is the reference
       state - ZERO O_ex, so the ledger closes with no extra term. The pool's C/H rises as a
       consequence, which is correct: stripping water out of a COH fluid drives the residue toward
       carbon saturation.
     PHASE SPLIT  pool -> {stays, ascends}.  CONGRUENT, fixed proportions on all three. Darcy moves
       a PHASE; there is no basis for fractionating elements at a permeability limit.
   ! The failure mode of the first rule - a parcel stripped of H arriving as bare carbon - is shut
     off by B.16, not by a clamp: as the pool dries of H2O, a_H2O falls, S_eff falls, dissolution
     stops. B.16 IS THEREFORE LOAD-BEARING FOR CONSERVATION, not only for realism, and is a
     prerequisite of 3.E rather than an independent refinement.
   ! THE O-NEUTRAL TRANSFER HAS A FEASIBILITY LIMIT. You cannot extract H2O from a fluid that has
     no oxidised hydrogen: removing dn_H at O_ex = 0 RAISES the floor -n_H/2 while n_Oex stays put,
     so a pure-H2 fluid at its floor is pushed outside. The bound is the hydrogen already in H2O
     form,  dn_H <= n_H + 2*n_Oex, and a_H2O -> 0 IS the fluid having no H2O to give. The activity
     scaling is not bolted on top of the dissolution rule, it is what makes the rule FEASIBLE.
   ! This also answers "can only part of the free H2O be partitioned per step" - malformed. Every
     kilogram that moves is FLUID, and fluid is the triple. The speciation limitation lives on the
     CAPACITY, never on the flux.
   ! THE BOUND-WATER DIFFUSION PATH NEEDS NOTHING (advdif -> smooth_tracer_H2O): it moves c_h2o
     only, which is pure H2O, hence zero O_ex by construction.

   ! TRANSIT, REACTION AND SPECIATION.  [settled 2026-09-05 after two corrections, both recorded]
     WHAT ncycles = 4 ACTUALLY DOES: A PER-FACE FLUX CAP, not a one-cell distance cap.
     allowed_flow = k*A*gP*rho*dt/eta is that face's Darcy capacity for this dt, and
     WaterMassExcessBelow is set once per column OUTSIDE the iz loop and never reset, so a parcel
     rises through as many cells as capacity allows in one step; what reaches the top is limited by
     the MINIMUM face capacity on the path - right for quasi-steady flow.
     ! CORRECTION 1: the claim that ncycles=4 "caps transit at ONE cell per step" was WRONG, and
       3.E.6 required limitflow_relperm on the strength of it. Both withdrawn.
     ! CORRECTION 2, THE ONE THAT MATTERS: multi-cell transit DOES destroy the chemistry, so the
       instinct to distrust it was right even though ncycles=4 was not the reason. The intermediate
       claim - that a passing parcel leaves elements in every cell, which then re-speciate at their
       own next solve - IS FALSE. Deposition is gated by the WATER SOLUBILITY logic and nothing
       else: oversaturated tracers EXSOLVE into the parcel, undersaturated ones ABSORB by at most
       (S - w), and A TRACER AT SATURATION EXCHANGES NOTHING. A permeable, water-saturated cell
       passes the parcel straight through untouched.
       ! AND THE CONTROL IS THE WRONG ONE. Under 3.E.2 the triple moves congruently, so C and O_ex
         transfer is SLAVED TO WATER STORAGE CAPACITY. Redox freezing does not need the rock to
         have room for water - it needs FERRIC IRON. The cell that is water-saturated and
         ferric-rich is exactly where the reaction belongs, and exactly the cell that receives
         nothing. Redox melting is blocked doubly, because its chain is
             deliver C + O_ex -> cell solve makes carbonate -> C(c_oxC) > 0 -> Tsol - 600 K
         (melting.f90, Dasgupta2007_carbon_Tmelting_adjustment, ALREADY IMPLEMENTED) and every link
         past the first needs a cell solve on delivered mass.
     THE FIX IS REACTIVE TRANSPORT, NOT A SPEED LIMIT. Throttling the flux to buy chemistry
     corrupts the flow rate, which was the objection to ncycles=4 in the first place. Operator-split
     instead - transport, then react - inside the existing bottom-to-top sweep. See 3.E.8, with
     3.E.7 kept as its degraded mode.
     ! LOCAL EQUILIBRIUM IS THE ASSUMPTION, and not a new one: fluid leaves cell k in equilibrium
       with cell k, enters k+1, re-equilibrates - the standard chromatographic-front model. NOT
       over-reaction when transit is fast, because the rock's finite capacity is enforced by the
       cell solve's own budget. It assumes kinetics fast against transit, which is what 3.D already
       assumes for the cell solve.
     ! phi_w CLAMPED [done 2026-09-05]. Under limitflow_relperm phi_w is built from
       (cellwfree + WaterMassExcessBelow), so a large incoming parcel could drive it past 1 - a pore
       space that does not exist - and phi_t feeds k = k0*(phi_t/phi0)**n, raising allowed_flow and
       admitting a larger parcel still. SELF-AMPLIFYING. Now phi_w <= 1, phi_weff <= 1 - totmelt,
       phi_t <= 1.
       ! NO MASS WAS EVER AT RISK: the parcel is assembled only from real tracer mass, the icycle-4
         cap is a min(), and WaterMassExcessBelow < 0 already error stops. THE FLUX LAW was
         unbounded, not the budget.
       ? min(allowed_flow, cellw), which the non-relperm branch applies, is deliberately NOT added
         under relperm - the parcel legitimately carries water from below.

 B.19 WHAT BELONGS INSIDE THE SOLVER   (2026-09-04)
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   ! THE CRITERION IS NOT O_ex. An earlier version read "a reservoir belongs inside iff its
     speciation carries excess oxygen". WRONG - melt H2O is the counterexample: it carries exactly
     ZERO O_ex and still belongs inside, because its AMOUNT is set by solve outputs.
         A RESERVOIR BELONGS INSIDE THE SOLVER IFF ITS CAPACITY DEPENDS ON SOLVE OUTPUTS FOR WHICH
         NO ADEQUATE LAGGED PROXY EXISTS.
       SOLID H2O  OUTSIDE. Capacity needs a_H2O only, which has a lagged proxy, and the solid is
                  the high-G reservoir B.16's implicit update exists for.
       MELT H2O   INSIDE.  Zero O_ex, but its capacity needs a_H2O AND it competes with melt H2 for
                  the same hydrogen at the same instant. No lagged proxy for that competition.
       MELT H2    INSIDE.  Needs f_H2, a solve output with no saturation analogue, and carries O_ex.
       FLUID      INSIDE.  Already is.
   ? WHY THE MELT'S PRIOR H2O/H2 SPLIT NEVER NEEDS TO BE KNOWN. Do not apply a law to the H2O
     sub-inventory; apply a JOINT capacity to the melt's total H,
         H_melt = S_H2O*a_H2O^n + K_H2*f_H2          (both in H2O-equivalent, B.17)
     Two intensive variables in, one total out; THE SPLIT IS AN OUTPUT OF THAT RELATION, NOT AN
     INPUT. Same argument as F.7 one level down - which is why no new tracer field appears.
   ! THE SPLIT-LEGITIMACY RULE, the general lesson and the reason this section exists:
         AN OPERATOR SPLIT IS LEGITIMATE ONLY WHERE THE BUFFER RATIO ACROSS IT IS O(1).
     B.16's G IS that ratio: ~1e2 for the solid, which is why the solid needs the implicit update.
     For the melt the ratio is melt-H over fluid-H, so plain iteration should converge - EXPECTED,
     NOT MEASURED. ! Two earlier attempts to split this coupling failed in the same direction, both
     by assuming a coupling was weak enough to lag. Check this rule first, not last.

 B.20 ONE MELT-WATER LAW FOR THE WHOLE MODEL          (2026-09-04, DECIDED - (b), implemented)
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   TOex computes the melt's H2O and OH- itself, from Sun & Yao (2026) against the solved P_H2O, and
   solves the fluid/melt hydrogen split (B.19, F.8). So every place that read a melt water
   SOLUBILITY as an independent input had to stop: solub_melt, thermochem_int('solubility')'s melt
   path, tra(WSOLPOS) on molten tracers, and the redistribution's S for molten tracers.
   ! LEAVING THEM IS A DOUBLE COUNT of exactly the B.5 kind: the melt would take water by
     solubility AND have its water re-decided by the solve, from a state the first step corrupted.
   (c) MEASURED 2026-09-04, and it changed the shape of the decision.
     ! FIRST FINDING: MOLTEN TRACERS NEVER USED THE TABLE. tracers.f90 calls Solub_melt
       unconditionally regardless of minimized_solubility - the thermochem_int path is SOLID only.
       So the comparison is against KATZ (2003), S = 12*P^0.6 + P, capped at its 10 GPa value.
     RATIO Sun & Yao (a_H2O = 1) / Katz, P 0.05-25 GPa, T 1200-2400 K:
         median 0.657 · within 1.25x 26.9 % · within 1.5x 45.5 % · mean |log10| 0.192
     RESTRICTED TO P <= 2 GPa, inside the S&Y calibration and with no clamp:
         median 1.110 · within 1.25x 70.0 % · within 1.5x 86.0 %, and below 0.5 GPa 1.01-1.05.
     ? SO THE DISAGREEMENT IS ENTIRELY IN THE EXTRAPOLATED REGION - one arbitrary choice against
       another. Above 2 GPa S&Y is frozen by the clamp while Katz rises to a cap its own source
       comment calls an "arbitrary cutoff (Rozel 2018)". NEITHER IS DATA.
     ? AND IT ONLY BITES IN A NAMEABLE REGIME. A fluid exists iff H_cell > phi*S, so the laws
       disagree on that verdict only inside a band, and every band sits at H >= 0.26 wt% of the cell
       (3 GPa, phi = 0.02: 0.524-0.751 wt%). At realistic mantle H of 0.01-0.1 wt% both say "no
       fluid"; at phi = 0 both say "fluid". Invisible except in WET, LOW-MELT-FRACTION cells -
       slabs, the wedge, hydrated lithosphere.
     ! THE FLAT CLAMP IS THE CONSERVATIVE DIRECTION, which is the argument to lean on rather than
       any claim about which extrapolation is right: lower capacity => melt saturates sooner =>
       FLUID APPEARS SOONER. Since the COH fluid carries up to 84 % of the Oex budget in reduced
       cases, spuriously KEEPING a fluid is a far safer failure than silently deleting the sink.
   ? DECIDED: (b). ONE law everywhere, carbon on or off, selected by toex_melt_H2O_law =
     'sunyao' | 'katz', DEFAULT 'sunyao'. Carbon off calls it at a_H2O = 1, which IS the pure-water
     saturation the legacy parameterisation represented - no second path, no branch on the carbon
     flag. 'katz' exists only to reproduce a pre-2026-09-04 run.
   ! WHAT THIS COSTS, accepted deliberately: at defaults a carbon-OFF run no longer matches the
     pre-change binary, so GATE 2a/3a FAIL BY CONSTRUCTION. => EVERY GATE 2a/3a RUN MUST SET
     toex_melt_H2O_law = 'katz'. Written down because a gate that silently stops being runnable is
     worse than one that fails.
   ! ONE PLACE ONLY: toex_melt_h2o_wt(P, T, aH2O) in volatilestuff. tracers.f90's molten branch
     calls it at a_H2O = 1; the solver calls it with a_H2O from its own speciation. THE SOLID IS
     UNTOUCHED and keeps its own table, scaled by B.16's activity.
   ! WITH 'sunyao' B.16's a_H2O^n EXPONENT IS NOT NEEDED FOR THE MELT: activity enters through
     P_H2O = a_H2O*P, and the law's own exponents (0.808 molecular, 0.449 OH) ARE the
     species-resolved activity dependence - measurably sub-linear (a_H2O = 0.5 gives 0.587 of
     saturated, not 0.5). B.16's ad-hoc n survives for the SOLID only.
   ? STILL OPEN, deliberately: the >2 GPa continuation. The flat clamp stands, justified on
     DIRECTION rather than as physics; melt-fluid miscibility above the second critical endpoint is
     NOT opened here. Revisit only if wet slab cells turn out to drive a result.

     B.12's r_deg and therefore of f_org. OH vs molecular H2O is a different case: both arrive by
     H2O + O(bridging) -> 2 OH, which conserves O and H, so that split moves no oxygen.

     magnitude between steps. Small, and NOT ignorable - it is a one-way reducing flux.

 ! 3.P.1  THE SOLVE NO LONGER NORMALISES BY THE BUDGET            [found+fixed 2026-09-06]
 ! =============================================================================================
   THE DEFECT. Every oxygen quantity was a SHARE of TOex, and TOex is SIGNED - a cell whose iron
   is partly metallic sits BELOW the FeO reference and has a legitimately negative budget. Divide
   by it and the sinks inherit the sign:
       ctx%maxsOex = 0.5*(Ms*X(3)/MMXOX(3))*MM_O/TOexg      -> NEGATIVE when TOexg < 0
   so invert_demand receives lo > hi, takes its `if (hi <= lo)` branch - written for the honest
   "zero-capacity sink, phase absent" case - and returns lo unchanged FOR EVERY fO2. Every sink
   freezes and the residual is a horizontal line.
   MEASURED on a failing cell (P 6.58 GPa, T 1903 K, TOex_in -9.76e-5, H_in 0, phi 0):
       ctx%TOex -9.76E-02   maxsOex -9.53E+01   minsOex -1.94E+02
       residual = +1.9311E+02 at EVERY f from -120 to +40, sOex pinned at +1.94E+02
   iters = 0, because toex_bisect_solve saw no sign change and returned immediately.

   FIX APPLIED - REMOVE THE NORMALISATION ENTIRELY. Sink demands and bounds are now ABSOLUTE
   MASSES OF OXYGEN in grams, and the one equation is
       sum(demands) = TOex          (was: sum(shares) = 1)
   The budget appears only on the right-hand side, so neither a negative budget nor a near-zero
   one can reach the brackets. An intermediate version normalising by |TOex| and solving
   sum(shares) = sign(TOex) also works and is a third the churn, but it keeps the near-zero
   pathology: shares were measured at 194x when |TOex| was small, which is the same factor the
   residual tolerance is then loosened by.
   TOUCHED: 8 division sites, 4 multiply-backs, the residual, the two bracket clips, the
   tolerance, and the writeback.
     - CLIPS are now 1e-10 OF EACH SINK'S OWN CAPACITY (ctx%clipS, ctx%clipM) rather than one
       fixed 1e-10, since a cell's oxygen capacity scales with its mass and iron content.
     - TOLERANCE is read as RELATIVE and scaled by max(|TOex|, 1e-6*capacity): the budget is the
       right scale when it is meaningful, the capacity floor stops a near-zero budget demanding
       impossible precision.
   ! THE ONE THING THAT DOES NOT CANCEL ANY MORE, and it cost a debugging cycle. The solve runs on
     a 1 kg REFERENCE CELL (MT = TOEX_MREF = 1e3 g). While the outputs were SHARES the reference
     mass cancelled on the way out - share x TOex(kg) is kg whatever MREF was - so the writeback
     never mentioned it. Absolute grams do NOT cancel: the writeback must divide by TOEX_MREF and
     multiply by the cell's real mass. Getting it wrong is SILENT - no error, no warning, just
     Oex_mean collapsing from 4.5E-02 to -4E-14 and fO2_mean moving 1.8 log units.
     THE TEST THAT CAUGHT IT: the dry case must reproduce the |TOex| version to roundoff. It read
     1.3 relative before the fix and 1.9E-10 after.
   ! toex_coh_state and toex_melt_fO2 still TAKE TOexg and no longer read it. Kept so call sites
     are undisturbed; the comments there say not to reintroduce a division.
   RESULT: wet failures 42 -> 8 and 61 -> 13 from this alone (the remainder was 3.P.3).

 ! 3.P.2  WHAT THE ABSOLUTE-MASS PATH COST US                       [closed 2026-09-06]

 ! 3.P.3  THE fex CLAMP LAUNDERED NaN INTO A NUMBER                   [found+fixed 2026-09-06]
 ! =============================================================================================
       cs%fex = cs%n_fluid*(2*XCO2 + XCO - 2*XCH4 - XH2)*MM_O/TOexg
       cs%fex = max(-1.d3, min(1.d3, cs%fex))

   toex_derive a FINITE 1000 - which then sailed straight through
       st%ok = ieee_is_finite(st%fex) .and. ...

     2. NEVER CLAMP A NaN. A non-finite fex is propagated as NaN so st%ok goes false and the cell

        negative O_ex means metallic iron instead. Recoverable, because the next solve re-partitions

      state Fe3+ does not, by many orders of magnitude. The form is a rate-limited exchange toward
      the cell solution, dOEX_applied = dOEX*(1 - exp(-dt/tau_redox_solid)), on the SOLID increment
      only. Two things to get right when it is built:
      ! IT IS A TRANSFER, NOT A PARTIAL APPLICATION. If the solid takes less oxygen than the solve
        says, the remainder must be somewhere - credit it to OFLUID explicitly. Applying a fraction
        of the solid's change while leaving the other reservoirs at their full new values loses
        exactly the un-relaxed amount, every cell, every step.
      ! A relaxing cell is deliberately OUT of equilibrium and therefore dirty by construction.
        Exclude it from 3.C.5's skip test, or tau silently becomes infinite.

 F.10 THE KEPPLER EXPONENT n PER SECTOR. 3.C.1 is wired with ONE constant, toex_aH2O_n = 1.0.
      The physics says it should differ by host mineral, because n is set by the INCORPORATION
      REACTION, not by the mineral as such:
        olivine        n ~ 1     hydrogarnet-type substitution, first order in f_H2O
        wadsleyite     n < 1     flattens toward a stoichiometric limit as the sites fill
        ringwoodite    n < 1     same reason, more strongly
        bridgmanite    n ~ 1     but the measurements are far weaker than for the UM
      ! THE THREE SECTORS ALREADY EXIST - the same split sf_H2O.jl uses for the storage
        parameterisations - so this is an array lookup, not new machinery. It supersedes G.5.
      ! WHY IT IS DEFERRED, NOT SKIPPED: at n = 1 the scaling is S*a, the standard first-order
        result, and it is the value the UM (where a_H2O = 1 by construction) is least sensitive
        to. n < 1 in the MTZ makes storage there LESS sensitive to a reduced fluid, so a constant
        n = 1 OVERSTATES the transition-zone response. That is the direction to remember when
        reading a result before this is built.
      ? A per-reaction n would be better still, since a single mineral hosts more than one
        substitution. Not worth it until the per-sector version has shown the effect matters.

===============================================================================================
 PART H - STANDING ASSUMPTIONS   (items are labelled I.n; cross-references use that)
===============================================================================================
 Things the model TAKES AS TRUE that are not theorems and are not enforced anywhere. Each one is a
 place a future result could be wrong without any code being broken. Keep this list current: an
 assumption that stops being listed is one nobody will re-examine.

 I.1  ONE fO2 PER CELL. Every sink in a cell shares a single oxygen fugacity, which is what
      collapses N unknowns to one. It assumes the cell equilibrates internally faster than it
      exchanges with its neighbours. At 1 cell = tens of km this is the strongest assumption in
      the model and the one the sub-grid heterogeneity of B.7 exists to soften, not remove.

 I.2  EQUILIBRATION IS INSTANTANEOUS. The writeback applies the full cell solution every step
      (3.D). No kinetic timescale anywhere - not melt-solid cation diffusion, not fluid-rock
      reaction. Deferred to F.9 by decision, not by oversight.

 I.3  ! THE FLUID'S AMOUNT IS PARAMETERISED BY HYDROGEN: n_fluid = molH_fluid/nH. A HYDROGEN-FREE
      C-O FLUID THEREFORE CANNOT EXIST. Measured cost at H = 0: fC_fluid 0.79 -> 0.00 at 1 GPa /
      phi 0.01, and 0.995 -> 0.000 at 0.5 GPa / phi 0.001, with the carbon going to carbonate. The
      REDOX answer survives (fO2 moves 0.005-0.02 log units, because carbonate and CO2 both carry
      +2 O per C); the MOBILITY does not. Closure candidates at the end of 3.G.

 I.4  THE SOLID'S BOUND WATER IS O_ex NEUTRAL. H entering a nominally anhydrous mineral is assumed
      to enter as OH against the Fe-as-FeO reference. It can also enter with a coupled Fe3+
      substitution, which would make it a redox term. Not modelled.

 I.5  THE MELT HOLDS NO GRAPHITE. w(TOEX_M_REC) = 0 by construction: precipitated reduced carbon
      always goes to the solid slot, and is folded onto the melt only when there is no solid at all.

 I.6  MELT H2O SOLUBILITY ABOVE 2 GPa IS A FLAT CLAMP. Sun & Yao's water half is calibrated to
      2 GPa (the 6 GPa envelope is the CO2 half), so everything deeper holds the 2 GPa value -
      41.7 wt% at a_H2O = 1. The fluid/no-fluid verdict at small phi rests on that one number, and
      the flat clamp is the conservative direction, not a measurement.

 I.7  NO SECOND CRITICAL ENDPOINT. Above ~3-4 GPa for basalt-H2O, melt and aqueous fluid are
      completely miscible and "is there a separate fluid?" has no answer. Nothing in the code knows
      the endpoint exists; the model's "no fluid" is the better of two available answers there.

 I.8  dH = 0 IN THE HIRSCHMANN H2 LAW. Set to zero deliberately - their 1400-1500 C range cannot
      calibrate it - so lnK carries no explicit temperature term beyond the volume one. Any use
      far outside that range is an extrapolation with no temperature dependence at all.

 I.9  SIX OUTER ITERATIONS ON THE FUGACITY-COEFFICIENT LOOP. The ZD09 non-ideality loop is run a
      FIXED number of times rather than to a tolerance. A solution is still reached without it,
      just less accurate; six is a judgement, not a convergence criterion.

 I.10 THE SOFTCAPS ARE CEILINGS, NOT MEASUREMENTS. Melt XCO2 at 0.3 and melt XH2 at toex_XH2max
      keep the solve bounded. Neither is a solubility datum, and a cell sitting on either cap is
      reporting the cap, not the chemistry.

 I.11 INITIAL CARBON HAS ONE SPECIATION. initCarbon_oex_per_C applies the same O_ex per carbon to
      the entire mantle, so a seeded run starts with no redox heterogeneity in its carbon at all -
      the opposite of what ingassing would produce.

 I.12 ! THE CARBON BUDGET PRINTED BY ComputeTotalWaterCarbonMass EXCLUDES FLUID CARBON (it sums
      OxCPOS + ReCPOS only). ComputeTotalOxygenCarbonMass is the complete one and runs immediately
      after it in melting.f90, so the STORED carbon_budget(4) is right and only the PRINTED
      intermediate line is short. Carbon moving into CFLUIDPOS reads as apparent loss on that line.

 I.13 THE COMPOSITION IS FIXED PER TRACER TYPE. X = p*EM_Xbs + (1-p)*EM_Xhz, with p from the tracer
      type alone. Melt and solid in a cell share one oxide composition, so nothing tracks how
      melting itself changes the basaltic component the solubility laws are calibrated against.

 I.14 ! THE WRITEBACK RENORMALISES THE CARBON SHARES (3.H). Carbon conservation is therefore a
      PROPERTY OF THE WRITEBACK, not evidence that the solver's carbon balance closed. A cell can
      conserve perfectly and still have the wrong split between graphite, carbonate, melt and
      fluid. toex_fC_dev is the only thing that shows it - watch it, do not assume it is small.

 I.15 THE OUTER SOLVE'S CONVERGENCE TEST IS ON OXYGEN ALONE. Carbon and hydrogen balances are
      inner solves, alternated inside the fugacity-coefficient loop, and their residuals do not
      enter the outer acceptance criterion.

 I.16 ! toex_illinois REPORTS BRACKETING, NOT CONVERGENCE. It falls out of MAXIT returning the
      last iterate with bracketed = .true., and its interval test is an ABSOLUTE width. Neither
      _solve_aC nor _solve_hydrogen reads even the bracketing flag. "It cannot diverge because it
      is bracketed" is true and beside the point: a bracketed solve that never converged is still
      a wrong answer, and it is the assumption behind every "the solver cannot fail" claim in
      PART D. See G.6.

 I.17 ! EVERY TOLERANCE IN THE INNER SOLVES IS ABSOLUTE, and the quantities they bound span
      fifteen orders of magnitude between a wet cell and a dry one. Both halves of G.6 are this
      one mistake. Any new inner solve should be posed in log space or given a relative test.

 I.18 ! aC_cap IS A THERMODYNAMIC UPPER BOUND, NOT A GUARANTEE THAT THE STATE EXISTS. The EMOG
      buffer says carbon activity cannot exceed it; nothing says the C-O-H speciation has a
      solution there, and for 33 281 solves per step it did not. Any bound used as a bracket
      endpoint must be CHECKED at that endpoint, not assumed valid because it is a bound.

 J.2  ! THE WATER VISCOSITY LAW WAS COMPLETELY INERT, AND THE PAR READ AS THOUGH IT WAS NOT.
      etalaw_water = 12 (Karato 2015) reads the PHASE-DEPENDENT arrays:
            etafac = max(1/(deta + (1-deta)*(min(W,20)/c0)**r), 0.0316)
            deta = phase_dependent_deta_water(i,isys)   c0 = phase_dependent_c0eta_water(i,isys)
      Those arrays are filled from the scalars in defaults_include.f90:
            phase_dependent_deta_water(:,:) = deta_water        ! deta_water default = 1.0
      and defaults_include RUNS BEFORE THE PAR IS READ. Setting deta_water = 0.01 in the par
      therefore never reaches the array, which stays at 1.0 - and at deta = 1 the law collapses:
            etafac = 1/(1 + (1-1)*(W/c0)**r) = 1   for EVERY water content.
      MEASURED: etalaw_water 12 vs 0, and deta_water 0.01 vs 1.0, both give BYTE-IDENTICAL eta,
      T and velocity fields. Water had no effect on viscosity at all. Instrumenting the branch
      showed it executing >2e6 times per step with deta = 1.000E+00 every time.

                     phase_dependent_r_etalaw_water = 40*0.75

 J.3  ! ACTIVATING J.2 STIFFENS THE MANTLE 100x, WHICH IS ALSO NOT WHAT IS WANTED. The law is
      normalised at W = c0eta_water: dry gives etafac -> 1/deta = 100, wet gives -> 0.0316.

      every cell sits at the DRY CEILING. Measured, with the arrays set correctly:
            depth 39.8 km   99.2x     98.8 km  142.8x     201 km  103.5x     406 km   98.1x
      The top cell is pinned at etamax = 1e26 anyway, so even its 11x weakening does nothing.
      ! THE REAL PARAMETER IS c0eta_water, which declares the water content at which eta0
        applies. With the mantle 9 orders below it, the law can only stiffen. Either c0 belongs
        at/below the model's dry value, or deta belongs at 1 so there is no dry-side stiffening,

 J.4  THE YIELD STRESS QUESTION ITSELF. With cohesion_eta and frictionCoeff_eta left at their
      BIG defaults the brittle branch never wins the min(), so the effective yield stress is
            Y(z,W) = (stressY_eta + P(z)*dstressY_eta_dp) * exp(-dstressY_eta_water*(W - c0))
      and with PressureDepRheology = .true. that P is the TOTAL PRESSURE IN Pa. At
      dstressY_eta_dp = 0.01, on the model's own reference profile:
            z(km)     12.8     39.8     68.4     98.8    131.1
            P(GPa)    0.400    1.243    2.152    3.146    4.217
            Y(MPa)    29.0     37.4     46.5     56.5     67.2      (at stressY_eta = 25 MPa)
      ! THE GRADIENT ALONE CONTRIBUTES 31.5 MPa AT 100 km - more than the whole 25 MPa target.
        No value of stressY_eta can give a 25 MPa lid at 100 km while dstressY_eta_dp = 0.01;
        with stressY_eta = 0 the base of the lid is still at 31.5 MPa. The depth-average over
        the top 100 km is stressY_eta + 17.4 MPa, so a 25 MPa AVERAGE needs stressY_eta ~ 7.6.
        Mobile/episodic/stagnant thresholds quoted in the literature are for a DEPTH-INDEPENDENT
        yield stress; comparing a nominal stressY_eta against them is comparing the surface
        value against an integrated one, and episodic behaviour is what sitting just above the
        transition looks like.
      ! dstressY_eta_water CANNOT BE CALIBRATED IN THIS SETUP, and that is why it resists. Its
        factor is exp(-0.2296*(W - 0.01)); at the measured W it spans
            top cell   W = 0.254 wt%  ->  0.946   (-5.4%)
            everywhere else  W ~ 0    ->  1.0023  (+0.2%, a STRENGTHENING, since W < c0)
        The knob multiplies a water content that is ~0 almost everywhere. To weaken by 50% at
        0.25 wt% it would need ~2.89, about 12.6x its current value, and would still act on one
        cell. Calibrate stressY_eta and dstressY_eta_dp first; water rheology is not a lever
        until the mantle actually holds water.

 ! G.6 DIAGNOSED: A DISCONTINUITY AT molH = 0, NOT AN aC/H ALTERNATION      [measured 2026-09-06]
 ! =============================================================================================
   WHAT WAS MEASURED, in cells with real hydrogen (molH_tot > 1e-6 mol):
     ~1.4-10.6 % of ideality evaluations have rH > 0.1, worst 1.63E+03. The distribution is
     BIMODAL - counts at rH>1e-6, >1e-3 and >0.1 are the SAME (13159/13159/13148) - so the
     balance is either exact or wrong by orders of magnitude, never in between. That is the
     signature of a BRANCH, not of slow convergence.
   WHICH BRANCH: every one of the 13 148 is the SATURATED hydrogen branch, on ITERATION 1, with
     lam = 1 and molH_fl = 4.66E-23 - i.e. the solve landed at essentially zero fluid, and the
     melt demand evaluated there is 1600x the whole budget.
   THE CAUSE is toex_carbon_demand's no-fluid shortcut:
         if (molH <= 0.d0) then ; nC = nC_melt ; return ; end if
         call toex_coh_reaction_step(...)          <- ONLY REACHED WHEN molH > 0
         if (.not. ok) nC = +Inf                   <- the veto that drives the ceiling walk
     VALIDITY DOES NOT DEPEND ON molH AT ALL - toex_coh_reaction_step takes no hydrogen argument.
     But the shortcut returns BEFORE the check, so at molH = 0 the veto never fires and aC sits at
     aC_cap, while at molH = 1e-30 it fires and walks aC down by decades. The hydrogen branch test
     evaluates at exactly 0 and the solve at epsilon: OPPOSITE SIDES OF A JUMP.
   HOW BIG, split deliberately (14 702 saturated solves):
         ACROSS molH = 0 (0 -> 1e-30*molH_tot)   max 4.0-5.2 decades   mean 1.72-2.05
         REST OF THE BRACKET (1e-30 -> full)     max 1.5-7.3           mean 0.05-0.44
     So 80-97 % of the aC "movement" is the artefact. Genuine sensitivity to the hydrogen split is
     a few tenths of a decade.
   ! THIS CORRECTS AN EARLIER 3.O CLAIM. I measured |dlog10 aC| = 1.94 mean across the bracket
     and concluded that freezing aC in the saturated branch would misstate it by two orders of
     magnitude. That measurement had the discontinuity inside it. The physical sensitivity is
     ~10x smaller, so the objection to freezing was much weaker than stated.
   ! THE MELT-H CLAMP IS A RED HERRING. 4981 clamps per step sounds alarming; measured, scH = 1.000
     and ZERO exceed 1 %, with 99.6 % in the undersaturated branch where the solve drives
     demand -> molH_tot exactly and the > comparison fires on the last bit. It is reporting
     floating-point dust, not inconsistency.

 WHAT MUST COME OUT OF THE REPOSITORY WHEN THE WORK IS DONE. Everything here was built to answer
 one question and has answered it. Scaffolding that stays becomes load-bearing by accident: the
 next person cannot tell a test harness from a model component, and every one of these has a
 namelist switch, which means a run can silently be in a mode nobody intended.
 ! THE RULE FOR DECIDING. A thing goes if it exists to PROVE something once. A thing STAYS if it
   exists to CATCH something every step - an invariant guard is not a unit test, it is part of
   the model's contract with itself, and the ones kept below are kept for that reason and no
   other. When in doubt, ask what a silent failure of it would cost.

 [ ] 6.1  TOex_stub_solve   (3.B.3, 4 references)
          Gather then scatter with no partition. It existed to prove the writeback is exactly
          conservative before there was a solver to write back, and GATE 3-0 passed. It cannot
          be a run mode: a par file with it on produces a model with NO CHEMISTRY and no warning.
          REMOVE the flag, the namelist entry and the branch in toex_solve_cells.

 [ ] 6.2  toex_probe_file and toex_probe.in/out   (3 references)
          The cross-check probe that runs toex_partition on a list of cases and writes them out.

          oxybarometer table is bound. Its job is done; the bench file is the permanent record.
          REMOVE, and if a standalone probe is wanted later it belongs in its own driver.

 [ ] 6.3  toex_proxy_audit   (3.L, 5 references)
