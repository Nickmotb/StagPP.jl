 3.1b WHERE THE 11.1 % GOES, and it is not a defect                  [new 2026-09-06]
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      144 of 1296 do not converge, and they are PERFECTLY LOCALISED:
          by TOex   1e-4: 0/432    1e-3: 0/432    1e-2: 144/432
          by TC     1e-6: 144/432  1e-2: 0/432    1e-1: 0/432

      Every failure is exactly (TOex = 1e-2) AND (TC = 1e-6): 144/144 of that combination, zero
      everywhere else. That is genuine capacity_exceeded. The rock's entire ferric capacity is
          maxsOex = 0.5*(Ms*X_FeO/M_FeO)*M_O ~ 8.8 g per 1000 g ~ 0.89 wt%
      so a 1 wt% budget cannot be held by iron alone, and with TC = 1e-6 there is no carbon sink
      to take the rest. No root exists; the solver correctly reports so and the cell keeps its
      previous partition. The mock branch of the historical rows had a different capacity, which
      is the whole reason those rows read 100 %.

 3.3  capacity_exceeded — an artefact THEN, physics NOW              [REVISED 2026-09-06]
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      Historically the ceiling was set by TABLE REACH (Rmax) rather than stoichiometry, and every
      one of the 96/242 flagged cases still had unused Fe2+ - an artefact, removed by extrapolating
      above the table. With the production table and a stoichiometric ceiling the remaining 144
      cases are the opposite: the budget genuinely exceeds what iron plus carbon can hold (3.1b).
      IN PRODUCTION a 1 wt% excess-oxygen budget with no carbon is not a physical mantle state; it
      is far more likely an upstream bookkeeping error (TOex inflated by double-counted oxygen).

 6. OPEN ISSUES
===============================================================================================
  1. XCO2max = 0.3 invented, but measured inactive across the sweep (worst raw/cap 0.994). Matters
     only near and above EMOG(D). See 5.3, 2.2.
  2. Low-P/T failures with real MAGEMin are reproduced by non-monotonic solid fO2 in the mock and
     repaired (§4), but the real cause is unconfirmed. Run real MAGEMin, watch for the warning.
  3. No precipitating Fe³⁺ phase. Believed unnecessary (§3.3) but untested above the tabulated
     range, where the extrapolation cannot capture a phase change MAGEMin would have found.
  4. Zhang & Duan (2009) is calibrated 673-2573 K and <=10 GPa; outside that the fluid is
     extrapolated and the code warns.
  5. All sweep results use the mock solid branch. Real MAGEMin now runs, so this is a re-run.
  6. RESOLVED FOR REPORTING 2026-09-03 — depth-driven self-reduction. Disproportionation conserves
     oxygen so the Frost mechanism sits at sOₑₓ = 0 and cannot DRIVE the solver, but metal is now
     harvested from the assemblage and reported (FeM_solid, split FeM_diss / FeM_disp). §1.8.
  7. Metal onset ~12 GPa vs a quoted ~250 km (~8 GPa), and the 20 GPa assemblage drops metal
     between two metal-bearing neighbours. Both unexplained. §1.8.
  8. Fux et al. (2024) benchmarked (§1.8). What survives the buffer fix: sb24 +0.06 to +1.86 too
     oxidised at their bulk Fe³⁺ (mean 0.99, was 2.48) and holds too little ferric at saturation
     (A 0.07 → 0.63; C 0.13 → 0.56). CAUSE: garnet's only ferric end-member is andradite, so Fe³⁺
     rides on calcium (x3 CaO → x8.9 ceiling), and opx has none. NOT the alloy — 5 GPa would need
     a(Fe) = 0.006. Fix is upstream: skiagite in gtmj, a ferric opx end-member.
  9. RESOLVED 2026-09-03 — reference buffers. FMQ and IW now come from sb24, the dataset producing
     the fO2 they are subtracted from. Removed a +1.7-to-+4.0 pressure-growing ΔFMQ error and
     stopped ΔIW putting metal-bearing rock above its own buffer. TOex.jl §4c, buffer_check.jl.
     G is computed in TOex.jl from Table C1/C3, not through LibMAGEMin.G_EM_function, so the
     buffers need no live handle and cannot segfault. Printed values cost <=0.0084 on FMQ,
     <=0.0012 on IW. Learned in passing:
       - SLB's `wu` is Fe4O4, not FeO (`pe` likewise Mg4O4), so the reaction is per TWO O2.
       - MAGEMin's `iw`/`qif` are UNUSABLE with sb: they call an end-member `iron` sb24 lacks and
         G_EM_function SEGFAULTS on unknown names. Stoichiometry is wrong too.
       - MAGEMin reads sb24's O2 Cp `b` from a negated slot (+6.91e-4 where HP have -6.91e-4).
         TOex.jl matches it deliberately: cancels in any offset, keeps ΔFMQ/ΔIW exact, moves the
         absolute buffer by 0.02-0.06.
       Both MAGEMin issues worth reporting upstream.
 10. sb24's iron redox scale sits ~1.4 log units ABOVE experimental 1-bar IW (-11.50 vs O'Neill's
     -12.90 at 1400 K). MECHANISM: MAGEMin's order-disorder slot (`cme`) is ZERO for every
     end-member, so Table C3's Landau term is never applied and wuestite is short R·ln5 per Fe²⁺
     = 1.398 log units, independent of P and T. It CANCELS in ΔIW (fayalite is short the same),
     which is why metal-bearing rock still lands physical — do NOT add it to IW alone.
     It does not cancel where the ABSOLUTE fO2 meets the HP98/Zhang & Duan fluid: 1.4 of the ~1.9
     offset in 1.5 is now accounted for. Fixing it means restoring the Landau term for ALL ferrous
     end-members — an upstream MAGEMin change.

 6.7  THE aC SOLVE — bisection to bracketed Newton
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       old   fixed 120 bisections on a 300-decade bracket, regardless of anything

 6.9  WHEN DOES A FREE COH FLUID EXIST? — the condition is a MASS balance, not a threshold
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A fluid exists when  H_cell > ϕ·S(P,T,X_H2O).  The ~40 wt% figure is the melt's SATURATION
   CONCENTRATION; it only appears as the melt's actual content when ϕ is small enough that a tiny
   melt must absorb everything. 3 GPa, 1700 K, TOex 1e-4, TC 1e-3, H = 0.05 wt%:
       ϕ = 0       fluid PRESENT, 100 % of H      subsolidus - nothing to dissolve into
       ϕ = 0.001   fluid PRESENT,  37 % of H      melt saturated at 31 wt%, cannot take more
       ϕ = 0.005   absent, melt at  9.78 wt%
       ϕ = 0.02    absent, melt at  2.43 wt%
       ϕ = 0.10    absent, melt at  0.48 wt%
   ⇒ THIS IS THE TEXTBOOK PICTURE, not an artefact: free COH fluid belongs to SUBSOLIDUS rock or to
     volatile-rich, low-melt-fraction settings. In a damp partially molten mantle the volatiles sit
     in the melt and no separate fluid is stable.
   ! ϕ = 0 works because nOxideTotal = 0 short-circuits the hydrogen split - nothing competes.

   THREE REAL CAVEATS, in order of how much they could move the answer:
     (a) THE SATURATION VALUE AT DEPTH IS EXTRAPOLATED. 41.7 wt% at 2 GPa is Sun & Yao's edge
         (their figures stop at 1 GPa) and the clamp freezes it for everything deeper. The
         fluid/no-fluid verdict at small ϕ rests on exactly that number.
     (b) ABOVE THE SECOND CRITICAL ENDPOINT (~3-4 GPa for basalt-H2O) melt and aqueous fluid are
         COMPLETELY MISCIBLE, so "is there a separate fluid?" has no answer. The model's "no fluid"
         is the better of the two available answers there, but it is not physics, and nothing in
         the code knows the endpoint exists.
     (c) THE VERDICT IS DOWNSTREAM OF THE OPERATOR SPLIT. In the coupled model H is what remains
         after the SOLID takes its bound water, outside the solve on a lagged activity (B.16,
         B.19). A small error in the solid's share flips the fluid between existing and not - the
         same buffer-ratio problem, now with a visibly binary consequence.

 7.16  WHY THE WET-RUN CELLS WERE FAILING                          added 2026-09-06
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   METHOD. Dump the inputs of the first failures, then sweep the residual across the whole bracket
   for one of them inside the model (the solid-fO2 table hooks make a standalone driver expensive,
   and the sweep needs the real ctx anyway). The shape of the residual answered it immediately in
   both cases - far faster than reading the solver.

   DEFECT 1 - NEGATIVE OXYGEN BUDGET.  Every failing cell had TOex_in < 0, H_in = 0, phi = 0,
   iters = 0. The sweep showed the residual FLAT at +193.11 across f = -120 .. +40, with sOex
   pinned at +194.11 = -minsOex. Cause: shares are of a SIGNED TOex, so a reduced cell inverts
   maxsOex/minsOex, invert_demand's `hi <= lo` branch (meant for an absent phase) returns lo
   unchanged for every fO2, and every sink freezes.
   FIX: normalise on |TOex|, carry the sign in the equation (sum(shares) = TOsgn).
   failures 42 -> 8, 61 -> 13.

   DEFECT 2 - NaN THROUGH THE CLAMP.  The remaining failures had a residual of -1.00 at f = -50
   and +1008 at f = -40. Instrumenting the term: nfl = 0, molHfl = 0, fex before the clamp = NaN,
   after = +1.0000E+03. 0 * Inf = NaN, and gfortran's min(1e3, NaN) is 1e3, so the clamp
   manufactured a finite value that passed ieee_is_finite - the guard written to catch exactly
   this. FIX: gate fex on n_fluid > 0, and propagate a non-finite fex instead of clamping it.
   failures 8 -> 1, 13 -> 3.

   ! A CORRECTION TO MY OWN REPORT. I had called dFMQ = +39.9 an "fO2 runaway". It is not. The
     worst cell is fO2 = -41.6 at T = 305 K, and the FMQ buffer at 305 K is -80: the DIAGNOSTIC'S
     REFERENCE is what is extrapolated, not the solution. One print of (dFMQ, fO2, T) settled it,
     and I should have looked before naming it.

   ! THE RECURRING SHAPE, now three for three (melt XCO2, the COH index, and fex): an expression
     is evaluated for a phase that does not exist in the cell, and something downstream - a
     softcap, a clamp - makes the nonsense look plausible. The rule that would have caught all
     three: gate on the phase, and never let a clamp run before the validity check.

   ! THE UNIT BUG THIS EXPOSED, and it is the interesting part. The solve runs on a 1 kg REFERENCE
     CELL. While the outputs were shares, that reference cancelled on the way out - share x
     TOex(kg) is kg whatever MREF was - so the writeback never had to mention it. Absolute grams
     do not cancel. Getting it wrong produced NO error and NO warning: Oex_mean collapsed from
     4.5E-02 to -4E-14, fO2_mean moved 1.8 log units, and every field file still looked plausible.
     WHAT CAUGHT IT was insisting the dry case reproduce the previous version to roundoff before
     comparing anything on the wet case. It read 1.3 relative; after the fix, 1.9E-10.
     THE FIRST WET RUN OF THE BROKEN VERSION LOOKED FINE - 0/2/7 failures, sensible iteration
     counts - and I nearly read its differences as a property of option B. Two of the numbers I
     briefly attributed to B (iters 15.4, window excursions 1.9E-03) were the unit bug.
   ! GENERAL: a change of units is not a change of formula, and the place it bites is wherever a
     ratio used to make the units cancel. Look for the cancellation, not just for the arithmetic.
